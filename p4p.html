Pound for Pound
Top 10 · 2026 Edition · The Blotter's Official Rankings
Vol. I · 2026 Documents Before · Scores During · Grades After

These rankings are built on one principle: the quality of the opposition, not the size of the stage. A fighter's P4P position is earned in the gym and the ring — not in a press conference or a promotional deal. We rank what we see. We explain why we see it that way.

1
1
No. 1  ·  Super Bantamweight / Jr. Lightweight
Naoya Inoue
The Monster  ·  Japan
There is no argument here. Inoue is the most complete fighter on the planet — undisputed at 118, undisputed at 122, and when he's cleaned out a division he moves up to find the next hardest challenge. That is the volunteer profile in its purest form. He doesn't wait for the sport to come to him. He goes to get it. The power translates at every weight, the timing is automatic, and the finishing instinct doesn't require a setup. He's #1 because nobody else in the world does what he does.
Volunteer Undisputed Multiple Divisions No Asterisks

2
No. 2  ·  Light Heavyweight
Dmitry Bivol
Russia  ·  WBA Light Heavyweight Champion
Bivol beat Canelo Alvarez when the entire industry said he couldn't. He didn't steal it — he earned it clearly, systematically, over twelve rounds against the biggest name in the sport. No asterisks on his résumé. No controversial decisions. No opponents who had something taken from them. He beats who's in front of him, defends his title, and doesn't manufacture the narrative. His spot at #2 is the cleanest argument on this list.
Beat Canelo Clean Undefeated No Asterisks

3
No. 3  ·  Lightweight
Shakur Stevenson
Newark, NJ  ·  WBC Lightweight Champion
The sport's system routes around Shakur Stevenson. He has sought the hard fights and the industry has found reasons those fights don't happen. That's not his problem — that's his credential. The style is suffocating: the distance management is generational, the jab lands like a setup and a finishing tool simultaneously, and his ability to make elite fighters look ordinary is something the division hasn't solved. When the avoided fighter designation is earned, it belongs here.
Avoided Fighter WBC Lightweight Seeks Hard Fights

No. 4  ·  Heavyweight
Oleksandr Usyk
Ukraine  ·  Unified Heavyweight Champion
The greatest heavyweight of his generation. The wins over Joshua — twice — and the unified championship are credentials that don't disappear overnight. What narrows the gap between him and the top three is accumulated cloud, not collapse. The Dubois I low blow ruling. Tonight's stoppage of Verhoeven with one second left in round eleven, a fighter who had been winning on the cards. Two fights with referee fingerprints on the result — Dubois I and tonight. Neither was clean. The body of work says top two. The recent work says #4.
Unified Heavyweight Champion Two Consecutive Controversies

5
No. 5  ·  Super Middleweight / Light Heavyweight
David Benavidez
Phoenix, Arizona  ·  The Mexican Monster
168 to 175 to 190 — and the power followed him every step. The body attack that broke Caleb Plant at super middleweight is the same body attack that cracked Zurdo Rodriguez at light heavyweight. When power diminishes moving up in weight, that's the rule. Benavidez is the exception. He's cleaning out a 32-pound range without losing a step, and the argument for placing him higher on this list gets stronger every time he fights. The standard P4P framework wasn't built for what he's doing.
32-Pound Range · Power Intact Multiple Division Domination

No. 6  ·  Light Heavyweight
Artur Beterbiev
Russia  ·  Unified Light Heavyweight Champion
Every professional win by stoppage. Every single one. The unified light heavyweight titles are held with the most devastating finishing rate in the sport. Beterbiev sits at #6 — not because he doesn't belong higher, but because Bivol is at #2. Both are the best at 175 pounds. Both deserve to be on this list. The ranking acknowledges the division has two legitimate No. 1s and an unresolved question that only one fight can answer. When that fight happens, one of them moves up.
Unified Light Heavyweight 100% KO Rate Bivol-Beterbiev Unresolved

No. 7  ·  Welterweight  ·  Avoided Fighter
Jaron "Boots" Ennis
Philadelphia, PA  ·  Welterweight Champion
He has sought Crawford. He has sought Spence. He has sought Haney. He has sought Tank Davis. Every single one of them found a reason the fight couldn't be made. That is not coincidence — that is the sport telling you exactly how dangerous this man is. The numbers confirm it: 89% stoppage rate, elite by every metric, from a city that produces fighters who don't back down. The avoided fighter designation isn't a story we're selling. It's what the evidence shows.
Avoided Fighter — Industry Consensus 89% Stoppage Rate

No. 8  ·  Super Flyweight
Jesse "Bam" Rodriguez
San Antonio, Texas  ·  Super Flyweight Champion
The smallest fighter on this list with one of the highest ceilings. Rodriguez operates at 115 pounds with timing, power, and IQ that don't belong at super flyweight — they belong on any P4P list in any era. The combination of youth, technical development, and title-level performances at a weight the casual audience ignores is exactly what this list is designed to surface. He's here because the quality of his work demands it, not because of name recognition.
Super Flyweight Champion Youth & Ceiling Both Elite

No. 9  ·  Bantamweight
Junto Nakatani
Japan  ·  Three-Division World Champion · 32–1 · Moved up to 122 for Inoue
Three-division world champion at flyweight, super flyweight, and bantamweight — and his most recent fight was a voluntary move up to 122 to challenge Inoue for the undisputed super bantamweight title at Tokyo Dome. He didn't wait for an easier fight at his natural weight. He went up four pounds to face the best fighter on the planet in the biggest fight in Japanese boxing history. He wasn't exposed. He made Inoue fall short with the jab in the early rounds and created genuine adjustment difficulty. The difference between outperformed and exposed is everything in how a career gets remembered.
Three-Division Champion Lost to #1 P4P — Not Exposed

No. 10  ·  Lightweight
Devin Haney
Las Vegas, Nevada  ·  Former Undisputed Lightweight Champion
Former undisputed lightweight champion. Built the résumé the right way — multiple title defenses, fought the available opposition, earned every belt in the division. The García loss is the complication on the record, but the circumstances around that fight are contested territory. A positive test for ostarine in the fight that defines García's case does not give García clean credit for the win. Haney's body of work at lightweight is legitimate. He's at #10 on those terms and those terms alone.
Former Undisputed Lightweight García Result Contested
On Radar  ·  Within Striking Distance
Jai Opetaia  IBF Cruiserweight
Elite at 200 pounds. Beat Mairis Briedis — a former undisputed cruiserweight champion — to take the title. The asterisk noted. Itching to move up. When he does, the conversation changes. He's one statement fight from forcing his way onto this list.
Oscar Collazo  WBO Minimumweight
The most compelling fighter under 115 in the sport. Puerto Rican. Dominant. The power at 105 pounds doesn't make sense until you watch him work. The casual audience hasn't found him yet. The Blotter has. He's building the case round by round.
