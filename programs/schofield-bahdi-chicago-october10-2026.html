The Read — Main Event
⚑ Blotter Flag · Narrative Spine
Gervonta Davis was placed in Champion in Recess status on June 26, and the WBA's answer was to order its No. 1 and No. 2 contenders into the same ring. Both are unbeaten. Neither has fought in more than a year: Schofield is 15 months out, Bahdi 13. One of them leaves Wintrust Arena with the belt.
Five Rounds Down, One Punch Left
Lucas Bahdi has one documented way to beat a boxer who is beating him: wait. Floyd Schofield has said publicly that he knows the plan. Whether knowing it is enough is the fight.

Floyd Schofield is 19-0 with 13 knockouts, 24 years old, and the WBA's top-ranked lightweight. His father, Floyd Sr., is his trainer. He had 180 amateur wins and multiple national titles. The professional résumé carries three marks. He stopped former IBF 130-pound champion Tevin Farmer in 78 seconds on June 28, 2025. In December 2024 he went 12 rounds for the first time against Rene Tellez Giron, won on cards of 118-109, 116-111 and 118-109, and was knocked down for the first time in his career late in round 11. And in February 2025 he withdrew days before a planned fight with Shakur Stevenson, hospitalized with an illness.

Lucas Bahdi is 20-0 with 15 knockouts, 32 years old, from Niagara Falls, Ontario, and he took the long way. He was a three-time Canadian champion and a 2016 Olympic alternate, turned pro in 2019, and won his first 11 fights by knockout. He paid for his early career himself. At 9-0 he fought in Cuernavaca, Mexico with no coach in his corner and a hand broken in camp, broke the other hand in round one, and still got the knockout. On July 20, 2024 he stopped Ashton Sylve, then 11-0 and 20 years old, in round six after trailing for five. Since then he has won three decisions covering 32 rounds: Armando Casamonica, Ryan James Racaza and veteran Roger Gutierrez, the last on August 23, 2025.

The tape is close. Schofield is 5'7" to Bahdi's 5'9", with a one-inch reach edge to Bahdi (67 and 68 inches). Both are orthodox, though Schofield switches. The real gaps are eight years of age and hand speed. The 2024 Sylve fight is the template for how Bahdi wins: he lost the early rounds, his coach told him he would get Sylve late, and he landed the shot.

Schofield told DAZN's Chris Mannix, "I just think that they're hoping I just jump in and he gets the same moment he had with Ashton." That is a fighter who has studied the film. The record complicates it. Against Tellez Giron, The Ring reported, he was rocked in round three, went back to the jab in round four, and later said, "I wanted to give the fans a show." He was dropped by a left hook after absorbing a right hand. In another interview he said, "I feel like I just give the fans the aggression side. I don't think they get to see me actually box." Whether that changes after 15 months out is a claim, not evidence. Neither man has lost, so neither has been stopped, and the upset path runs through one clean shot.

Judge's Chair4
How This Gets Scored
Decision Risk: MEDIUM

Judges weigh four things in order: clean, effective punching; effective aggression; ring generalship; defense. Schofield's speed and volume score well on the first. Bahdi's single counters score only if they land clean, and a round he spends waiting looks like a round he lost. Close rounds tend to tilt toward the better-known fighter, which is Schofield.

The judges are Glenn Feldman, Ed Kanner and Rocky Young, per Golden Boy's announcement. The referee had not been announced when this program was built.

Score it yourself: if Schofield is still boxing off the jab at round six, it is his card. If he is in the pocket by round five, start giving Bahdi rounds. If the jab is still working at round nine, the late-round question is answered.
What This Fight Settles
Both men are on the Blotter's prospect watch, and both are looking to cross over. This is not a flashy fight, but it is the kind real boxing people watch for direction: which of these two is built for the top of the division? We are not asking for a label. We are asking whether a viewer can see three things. He wins the late rounds, seven through twelve, against a live opponent. He solves a problem mid-fight instead of repeating what is not working. And he does both against a ranked, unbeaten opponent on a major platform. It is possible that neither answers it. This program says so if that happens.
Three Things To Show · Schofield
Can he hold the plan for twelve?
1. The distance. He has been 12 rounds once, and it ended with him on the canvas. Watch rounds seven through twelve, and whether the jab is still working at nine.
2. The discipline. His corner told him not to brawl with Tellez Giron and he traded anyway. Watch the first clean shot Bahdi lands: does he reset or trade?
3. The schedule. Fifteen months out, after the February 2025 Stevenson withdrawal. Watch how he looks in round one.
Three Things To Show · Bahdi
Is Sylve a method or an event?
1. The method. One shot after five lost rounds. Watch whether he creates openings, with feints, the body and pressure, or only waits for one.
2. The rounds. Three decision wins since Sylve, and no knockout. Watch whether he is winning any round without the big shot.
3. The stage. His first title fight. Watch whether the moment changes his rhythm.
How the result reads
  • Schofield wins clean, jab alive late: the question is answered. The next test is a top-of-division name.
  • Schofield wins but is hurt or dropped again: he has the belt and the question stays open. The thesis becomes "fragile when he trades."
  • Bahdi wins inside the distance: Sylve was not an accident, and the method is confirmed twice.
  • Bahdi wins on the cards: he answered the harder question, that he can win rounds without the one shot.

The Blotter Spotlight — Prelims
⚡ Scout's Chair — The Fighter You Need to Know
Grant Flores
13-0 · 9 KOs · Thermal, CA (Coachella Valley) · 21 · Orthodox · Trainer: Robert Garcia

Grant Flores turned pro on February 23, 2023, at 18, and he is 13-0 at 21 with nine knockouts, a 69% stoppage rate.5 Golden Boy describes an "aggressive, fan-friendly style and knockout power." In his last fight, on March 14, he knocked down Rashid Stevens three times on the way to a unanimous decision (77-72, 78-71, 79-70). He has now gone eight rounds twice, against Courtney Pennington and Stevens.

Tonight he steps up in length and in danger. He faces Mikey Dahlman, 19-2 with 17 knockouts, 34 years old, from Chicago Heights, in his first scheduled ten-rounder. Dahlman has stopped 89% of his wins, and he is the home fighter. But both of his losses came early: round 2 against Fidel Monterrosa Munoz in April 2022, and round 1 against Michael Zerafa in August 2025. He is a puncher who has been hurt in the first two rounds, and those are the rounds that matter.

The question is whether Flores can turn a good eight-round résumé into a ten-round win over a dangerous veteran in his backyard. Watch the first two rounds, whether he boxes behind his height (listed at 6'1" to 6'2") or trades, and whether he can finish a hurt man. A stoppage or a dominant decision moves him toward a ranked opponent. A decision after being hurt, or a loss, turns the file into a question about his chin and his corner's plan. We could not find his amateur background, so this program prints no number.

Flagged · Prelim · Super Bantamweight · 8 Rounds
Asa Stevens
7-0-1 · Waianae, HI · 25 · Southpaw · 5'5" · vs. Cristian Williams (14-0, 8 KOs)

Stevens won gold at the 2018 Youth World Championships and the 2019 National Golden Gloves, and signed with Golden Boy as a teenager; Oscar De La Hoya predicted he would reach the top of the division. Eight fights in nearly six years is not that path. He has five decision wins and two stoppages, a split draw with Alvarado in April 2025, and he has not fought in 18 months. This is the card's best fit for the real grind: a once-hyped amateur whose climb stalled.

Williams is 32, from Chicago, and unbeaten at 14-0, but we could not verify his opposition. Stevens needs a clear win, not just a win, and he needs to look like the fighter he was projected to become.
