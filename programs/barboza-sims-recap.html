The Game Story
Levels to This.
The fight on paper was a coin flip. Two contenders coming off losses at 140, meeting at welterweight for the first time, both needing a win to stay relevant. That is what the odds said. What Barboza said with his feet, his jab, and his right hand was something else entirely.
From the opening bell, Barboza controlled the distance. Sims came in as a southpaw and switched to orthodox partway through round one — an adjustment Barboza never had to make, because he made Sims uncomfortable first. Shorter by an inch but carrying the longer reach, Barboza used that reach to establish a sharp jab that grew more damaging as the rounds accumulated. By the midpoint of the fight, visible swelling had formed under Sims's right eye — the physical ledger of a man being beaten to the punch, round after round.
Sims's path to winning required getting inside. He never did. Barboza's footwork and combination timing negated Sims's single biggest weapon — his ability to work effectively at close quarters, as he showed in his 12-round war with Oscar Duarte last August. That fight never happened Saturday night. This was a different kind of fight: clean, controlled, one-sided, and occasionally boring enough for the Honda Center crowd to let them know it. The boos were real. So was the scorecards.
In round 11, a head clash opened cuts on both fighters — Barboza from the hairline, Sims near the right eye. Neither cut changed the trajectory of a fight that had been settled for six rounds. In the 12th, Sims landed his best shot of the night — a crushing right to Barboza's face. Barboza absorbed it, looked at Sims, and came straight back at him. In the final minute he snapped Sims's head back with right hands to close the chapter.
⚡ The Moment
Round 12 · The Last Right Hand.
Sims landed the best punch of his night — a right hand that would have rocked a lesser opponent. Barboza didn't blink. He walked through it, said "I told you there were levels to this," and spent the final minute putting the exclamation point on a performance that was never in doubt. The moment wasn't the punch that landed. It was the answer that came after it.

Player Verdicts · Box ScoreStory First Blueprint
Player Verdicts
Exceeded
Arnold Barboza Jr.
The pick'em odds implied an even fight. Barboza delivered a dominant welterweight debut, controlling every dimension — footwork, distance, jab, body work, and punch timing. One judge gave him every round. He came in longer-reaching despite being shorter, used that reach precisely, and denied Sims the close-quarters battle that was his only path. The post-fight callouts of Garcia and Haney were not arrogance. They were a man who just proved he belongs at that level making his case while the room was still listening.
Missed
Kenneth Sims Jr.
Came in with a clear gameplan — get inside, work close quarters, make it a war. Executed none of it. Sims never made the effort to close the gap and appeared to be waiting for a fight that Barboza had no intention of giving him. The best punch of his night came in round 12 — too late, and answered immediately. His southpaw-to-orthodox switch in round one suggested uncertainty that never resolved. The crowd's frustration was not with Barboza's performance. It was with Sims's lack of urgency in a crossroads fight.
Exceeded
Oscar Collazo
Co-main event. Unified WBA/WBO strawweight champion forced Jesus Haro's corner to stop the fight after six rounds — Haro did not answer the bell for round 7. Collazo invested heavily in the body from round two onward, turning a mobile, fleet-footed opponent into a stationary target by the sixth. Fourteen fights, eleven stoppages. The pattern is not a coincidence. Collazo is building one of the most underrated championship résumés in the lighter weight classes.
Delivered
Alexis Rocha
Returned after 14+ months out and defeated Joseph "JoJo" Diaz Jr. by unanimous decision (100-90, 98-92, 97-93) at super welterweight. Rocha looked sharp for a fighter with that long a layoff. His post-fight appearance in the ring to call out Barboza was the shrewdest move of the night — Golden Boy has both fighters, and the matchmaking conversation began immediately.
Exceeded
Gabriela Fundora
Retained her undisputed flyweight championship with a sixth-round TKO over Viviana Ruiz Corredor. Fundora scored a knockdown in round four with a right hook/left uppercut combination, then broke Corredor down on the ropes with sustained body shots in round six to force the stoppage. Undefeated. Undisputed. Still the name to watch in women's flyweight.
Exceeded
Joel Iriarte
Retained the WBA Continental Gold welterweight title with a sixth-round TKO over Rock Dodler Myrthil (17-3-1). Myrthil briefly staggered Iriarte with a right hand in round two — a test the 22-year-old prospect answered cleanly, taking control behind his jab and heavier combinations through the middle rounds. Undefeated, 10-0 with 9 stoppages. Watch the name.

Legacy

Barboza enters the welterweight division with a statement. He is 33-1 with 11 knockouts, holds the WBO Global title at 147, and has publicly called out Ryan Garcia, Devin Haney, and Teofimo Lopez — the man who beat him last May. Alexis Rocha stepped into the ring immediately after the final bell to ask for the fight. Golden Boy has both. That fight is the most logical next step and may be announced within weeks.

For Sims, the road gets harder. He is now 22-4-1 — four losses, three of them in significant fights. He was outboxed, not outpunched. He was out-schemed, not out-hearted. Whether that is a meaningful distinction depends on what adjustments he makes next. His corner — family-trained — has been questioned publicly in the aftermath. Whether new eyes in the gym change the trajectory is the only open question worth asking on his behalf.
The Anaheim card as a whole was a Golden Boy statement of depth. Collazo is becoming one of the most dangerous minimumweight champions in the sport. Fundora is undisputed at flyweight. Iriarte is a 22-year-old prospect with nine stoppages in ten fights. Oscar De La Hoya's promotional house has star power at the top and real talent building beneath it. That infrastructure matters when the big fights need main events to support them.
What Comes Next
Barboza's stated targets: Ryan Garcia (WBC welterweight) · Devin Haney · Teofimo Lopez (rematch at 147). All three are legitimate conversations. Garcia is the most commercially attractive. Rocha is the most logistically immediate. Lopez is the most personally motivated. Watch which one Golden Boy announces first — it tells you exactly how they see Barboza's value on the marquee.

Arnold Barboza Jr. · To Kenneth Sims Jr. · Round 12 · Honda Center · Anaheim · March 14, 2026
Barboza didn't say it for the crowd. He said it in the ring, to the man across from him, in the final round of a fight that had already been decided. That is the difference between a man performing and a man competing. He came to Anaheim to prove something — not to the audience, but to himself and to the division. The scorecards agreed. So did the watch party of names he called afterward. The welterweight conversation just got more interesting.
