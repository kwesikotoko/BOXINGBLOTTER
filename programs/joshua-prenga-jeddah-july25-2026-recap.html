Game Story
⚑ Blotter Flag · Narrative Spine
The program's watch condition asked whether Prenga's pressure would find Joshua's chin early. It did — twice, inside the opening minute — before Joshua reversed the entire fight in the second.
The Chin Question Is Answered. A New One Just Opened.
Anthony Joshua, 36, survived two first-round knockdowns to stop Kristian Prenga, 35, in the second — a result violent enough to settle one debate about Joshua and start another.

Prenga answered the bell like a man who'd read the scouting report and decided the size gap didn't matter. A right hand and a follow-up right uppercut dropped Joshua just 19 seconds in — the same kind of opening-minute chin check Daniel Dubois had already put on record. Joshua rose unsteady and went down again before the round ended; a third sequence, where Prenga bundled him to the canvas, was ruled no knockdown. There is no more asking whether the chin is a real question. Joshua got hurt, badly, by a fighter with one professional loss on a mid-tier resume, and it happened twice in three minutes.

What was never in question is the power. Round two answered that part just as clearly. Joshua reset behind the jab, found range with a right hand, then landed two more clean rights that put Prenga down against the ropes for good. Referee Luis Pabon waved it off at 2:43. Prenga's own output told the story of the reversal: 13 of 46 punches landed in the first round, per CompuBox, and just 3 of 17 before the finish in the second.

The real question walking out isn't whether Joshua can hurt someone anymore — it's whether he can get there before someone hurts him first. Against a live puncher at this level, the two-time champion needed a recovery, not a coronation. On tape, he also looked heavy-legged and slow to close in the buildup to the first knockdown — seven months removed from a car crash that killed two members of his team, leaning on a frame that reads more built for size than tuned for boxing. That's not a settled verdict, but it's a live thread. "There's got to be a higher power involved," Joshua said after. "You fall down, you stand up and keep going." The win keeps the long-rumored Tyson Fury fight on track, with Fury having stopped Mariusz Wach in Thailand the night before — but it ships with more doubt attached than a clean knockout usually carries.

Player Verdicts
Anthony JoshuaMissed
Called by KO/TKO, and that's exactly what happened — but the program's real watch condition was whether the Dubois-style chin issue would hold up, and it did not. He got hurt early and badly by a fighter with one loss on a limited resume. The recovery was real and it was earned, but the durability question is now more open heading into Fury, not less.
Kristian PrengaExceeded
Not the pick, but by a wide margin the story of the night. A 20-1 fighter built on limited opposition put a two-time unified champion on the canvas twice and had him looking lost for most of round one before finally running out of ring.
Hamzah SheerazMissed
Called by KO/TKO. Delivered a majority decision — one judge had it even at 114-114 — against a voluntary challenger coming off a loss. CompuBox backs the scorecards (112-98 total, 97-56 power) but also shows a fighter whose volume collapsed down the stretch: 3 of 14 landed in round 10, 5 of 27 in round 11, 4 of 32 in round 12. Retains the title; doesn't retain the "impressive" framing he was chasing pre-fight.
Josh KellyDelivered
Called Decision, won by unanimous decision (114-113, 115-112, 115-112) in a fight that cost him cuts and a point deduction for blows to the back of the head. The scorecards read comfortable; the CompuBox numbers (97/437 to Agyarko's 87/352) barely separate the two, with a real power-punch dip in rounds 7-8 right as Agyarko's connect rate spiked. Composure carried him through — whether the engine holds up against Boots Ennis or Sebastian Fundora is now a real question, not a settled one.
Jacob BankExceeded
Called Decision as a "narrow lean." Delivered a clean shutout, 80-72 on all three cards, with a jab that had August marked under the eye by round two. The fluidity and footwork draw a real Caleb Plant comparison — same frame, same weight class, similar rhythm. One open question worth tracking: how many southpaws is that resume actually built on.

Anthony Joshua
The Fury Fight Is On. So Is a New Question.

Joshua's chin question is now a bigger story than it was walking in, not a smaller one — he's heading to Fury off a fight where he was seconds from an upset loss, not a clean statement win. On tape he also looked heavier and slower to close than the boxer who beat Jake Paul in December, a conditioning thread nobody had flagged pre-fight and worth tracking as the Fury fight approaches.

Kristian Prenga
A Loss That Raised His Price

Despite falling short, Prenga likely raised his stock more than any winner on this card raised theirs — a 20-1 fighter with limited opposition behind him nearly took out a two-time unified heavyweight champion.

Jacob Bank
The Card's Real Statement Win

Not any of the three title-fight winners, but Jacob Bank might be the fighter this card did the most for — a Blotter-flagged prospect who looked the part against a live, unbeaten opponent, with one clean, specific question (southpaws) standing between him and a real push toward Sheeraz.
