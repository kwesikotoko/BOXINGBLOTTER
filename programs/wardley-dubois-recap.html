The Night

What Manchester Produced

The tagline was "Don't Blink." Manchester did not blink. It produced a legitimate Fight of the Year candidate in the main event, the biggest upset in British boxing in years on the undercard, a dominant welterweight performance from a fighter now knocking on the door of a title shot, and a Prospect Watch entry that went seven rounds when the call had him finishing by round four. This card gave the Blotter more to file than any card since Inoue-Nakatani.

The headline stories are two. Dubois answered the one question that mattered about him — the psychological question — by getting up twice and winning. And Zak Chelli, the fighter this program covered before anyone else was writing about him, stopped a two-time world champion at 33-1 odds in the final round. Those two stories define the night. Everything else is filed in its proper place below.

Main Event

Dubois TKO11 Wardley — New WBO Champion

Wardley came out fast and landed the right hand in the opening moments. Dubois went down in round 1. The Co-op Live erupted. Wardley pressed. Then Dubois went down again in round 3 — Wardley's power was landing. And then something happened that Dubois's critics had insisted would never happen: he got up. Both times. And he came back.

The fight turned when Dubois settled into his jab. The analytical blueprint from the pre-fight program — Dubois uses his jab, controls range, doesn't let Wardley get inside — played out in the middle rounds exactly as called. Dubois began landing cleaner, heavier, and with better angles. Wardley never stopped coming, which is the truest thing that can be written about him, but he was taking more than he was giving.

"It was a war. We came through the sticky moments. Thank you, Fabio, for that." — Daniel Dubois, post-fight

Going into round 10, the ringside doctor examined Wardley before the bell rang. They let it continue. Wardley went out for the 10th and kept fighting. Dubois finished it in round 11 — pinned Wardley on the ropes, unloaded a sustained attack, referee Howard Foster stopped it. Wardley never officially went down. He absorbed an extraordinary amount of punishment over 11 rounds and stayed upright throughout, which is a fact worth documenting separately from the loss.

Dubois is 23-3, 22 KOs. Two-time world heavyweight champion. Wardley is 20-1-1, 19 KOs. Frank Warren confirmed Wardley holds a rematch clause. That fight, if it happens, will sell itself.

Spotlight Vindication

Zak Chelli · The Story the Market Missed

The pre-fight Spotlight section ran under the headline "The Man Nobody's Covering." The argument was specific: Chelli's amateur background — victories over Ben Whittaker and Lewis Richardson before either won Olympic medals — was the buried data point that the pre-fight coverage was ignoring. The market had him at 33-1 to win by knockout. Some outlets reported odds as high as 35-1.

The fight unfolded close to exactly as the Spotlight described. Chelli stayed behind a high guard, worked the body throughout the middle rounds, kept walking Morrell down when Morrell wanted to move and pick. Morrell controlled long stretches behind the jab — the cleaner work belonged to him through the early and middle rounds. He built an apparent lead. Then he took his foot off the gas, the way the pre-fight program described as his documented tendency after hurting opponents.

"I said I'd shock the world, did I not? And I did it. I'm a man of my word. Give me anyone." — Zak Chelli, post-fight to The Ring

In the 9th, Chelli landed a right hand that hurt Morrell — the same looping overhand right the Spotlight identified as his primary power weapon. Morrell survived the round. He came out for the 10th believing he had done enough. Chelli landed it again, landed the follow-up combination, and referee Darren Sarginson stopped it at 2:24 of round 10. Morrell had never been stopped in his professional career before this fight.

Chelli is now 17-3-1, 9 KOs, ranked No. 10 at 175 lbs by The Ring. He has called out every champion in the light heavyweight division. His words post-fight: "Benavidez didn't do that to him. I did." That is not wrong.

Supporting Card

The Rest of the Results

Rafferty TKO6 Essuman Welterweight · 10 Rds
Rafferty (27-0-1, 18 KOs) was the better fighter before the cut changed the equation. Essuman's corner stopped it after round 6 — a bad cut was affecting him throughout and the corner made the call. Rafferty was winning on the cards regardless. Dominant welterweight debut for the 30-year-old from Shaw. The Lewis Crocker IBF title fight is the logical next call. Essuman at 37, now 22-3, is at the end of this level. His two recent losses came to Catterall and now Rafferty — both legitimate fighters, both stoppages. The clock is visible.
Rea TKO4 Cameron LHW · WBA Intercontinental
Rea (22-2, 10 KOs) applied pressure throughout and repeatedly landed the harder shots. The turning point was a clean left hand that dropped Cameron heavily in round 4 — Rea followed up after Cameron rose and the referee stopped it. Cameron (24-8-1, 10 KOs) is 37 and has now been stopped for the third time in his last six fights. Rea wins the WBA Intercontinental belt at light heavyweight. A credible domestic scene performance — next step is seeing where the WBA ranks him and what gets made.
Gwynne MD over Majid SLT · WBA International
Scores: 96-94 twice, 95-95. Gwynne (19-4-2) wins by majority decision — one judge saw it even. Majid (16-1) loses his unbeaten record in his first real test. The Blotter called Majid to watch and noted him as the lesser-known undefeated fighter on the card. The loss doesn't disqualify him from the conversation but it does change it — the WBA ranking he was building toward now requires rebuilding from a different position. Gwynne at 35, 1-2-1 in his last four, took the opportunity and made something of it. Honest result. Majid has work to do.
Jalolov TKO7 Smakici Heavyweight · 8 Rds
Jalolov (17-0, 15 KOs) made his UK debut and did not press for the early finish. Corner stopped it after round 7. The pre-fight call had him finishing by round 4 — that did not happen. He appeared to pace himself rather than hunt the stoppage. Whether that is conditioning, tactical patience, or something else is filed with the Prospect Watch update below. The Itauma callout remains public. This performance did not close that analytical question — a 7-round corner stoppage against a limited opponent does not tell us what a fight with Moses Itauma would look like.
Ummataliev KO2 Drabik Super Middleweight · 6 Rds
Ummataliev (2-0) finishes quickly on the Manchester card. Two professional fights, two stoppages. No extended sample yet to analyze. File the result and move on.
Flood · Hamilton-Allen Debuts Super Welterweight · 4 Rds
Flood (2-0) wins by points, 40-36 over Darby. Hamilton-Allen (2-0) wins by points, 39-37 over Goulding. Both picked up their second professional wins in front of a large Manchester PPV audience. Four-round fights on a card this size are a television audition as much as anything else. Both passed. Neither result requires more analysis at this stage.

Bakhodir Jalolov Vol. I · Entry No. 6 · Active
Updated record: 17-0, 15 KOs. UK debut complete. Jalolov did not go for the early finish the Blotter projected — the call was TKO R1-4, the fight went to a corner stoppage in round 7. He appears to have paced himself. Whether that reflects tactical patience at 254.9 lbs, a conditioning choice, or simply a less-than-motivated opponent who needed time to break down is not answerable from one fight. The Itauma callout is still public. A corner stoppage of a 21-3 opponent in 7 rounds does not accelerate or confirm that conversation either way.
Entry status: Active. Itauma question remains open. Next fight is the filing that matters.
David Morrell Jr. Watch Candidate · Status Changed
Updated record: 12-2, 9 KOs. Two losses in 15 months. First career stoppage. The pre-fight note on Morrell read: "This is no longer a rebuild story — it is a career crossroads." That was written before the result. The result confirmed it.

The Blotter did not formally file Morrell because he was not undiscovered. The rationale was that tonight's performance would determine whether the formal filing made sense. It does not. A two-time world champion who has been stopped for the first time in his career, against a 33-1 underdog, on 3 weeks' notice, after losing to Benavidez — the analytical question is not about his ceiling anymore. It is about whether he finds the rematch with Benavidez and whether the chin question that this loss raises is structural or situational.
Watch Candidate note updated. Formal Prospect Watch filing will not proceed. Morrell's story is a different chapter now — career inflection, not emerging ceiling.
