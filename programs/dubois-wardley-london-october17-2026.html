The Read — Main Event
⚑ Blotter Flag · Narrative Spine
The first fight was two fights in one: Wardley's early power, then Dubois's long control until referee Howard Foster stepped in during round 11. The rematch is about which half repeats.
Ten Seconds, Then Eleven Rounds
Dubois took the WBO belt from Wardley on May 9 and makes his first defense against the man who dropped him twice. Wardley invoked his rematch clause within days.

Dubois is 23-3 (22 KOs), a 96% stoppage rate, and the losses are the part of the résumé nobody skips. Joyce stopped him in round 10 in 2020 and broke his orbital. Usyk finished him in round 9 in 2023 and round 5 in 2025. He has had four trainers. Don Charles took over in 2023, split with him in August 2025, Tony Sims followed, and Charles returned in January 2026. Advisor Sam Jones left on August 4, 2026.

Wardley is 20-1-1 (19 KOs), a 95% stoppage rate, with no amateur bouts and four white-collar wins behind him. He drew with Clarke over twelve (split decision, March 2024), stopped Clarke in round 1 of the rematch, stopped Huni in round 10 in June 2025 while behind, and stopped Parker in round 11 in October 2025. Ben Davison is his head trainer, with Robert Hodgins as co-trainer.

The tape is a mirror: same height, reach and stance. What separates them is weight and age. Both made weight at career highs for the rematch, per BBC, and Wardley is up about six pounds from the first fight while Dubois is up about two.

The Blotter's inference, labeled as one: Wardley trailed through the middle rounds against technical boxers (Huni, Clarke in the first fight, and Dubois). The cause is unresolved. It could be heavy early output, cumulative damage to the nose and eyes, or conditioning. Wardley said himself that he had two clear chances to finish early and got too comfortable (Sportsnaut). Dubois claimed Wardley was overweight and under-trained, and Wardley called that "bizarre." Treat all of it as claims, not findings.

The Blotter Spotlight — Jalolov
JalolovWith his Olympic gold medal · Paris, 2024
⚡ Scout's Chair — The Fighter You Need to Know
BAKHODIR JALOLOV
17-0 · 15 KOs · Sariosiyo, Uzbekistan · 32 · Southpaw · 6'7" · 81" Reach

Jalolov is the most decorated amateur on the card. He won Olympic gold at Tokyo 2020, beating Richard Torrez Jr. by unanimous decision in the final, and again at Paris 2024. He is a two-time world champion (2019 and 2023) and a three-time Asian champion (2017, 2019, 2021). The Ring's 2021 profile put his amateur record at 247-17. He turned pro in 2018 and has seven first-round knockouts.

The pro side is thinner than the résumé. He is 17-0 with 15 KOs, an 88% stoppage rate (15/17). He returned to pro boxing in April 2025 with a ten-round decision over Shevadzutskyi, his first fight to go the distance, then beat Kudukhov (UD8, October 2025) and Smakici (RTD7, May 9, 2026). The Ring says he has boxed nobody near the heavyweight top 50, and the WBO and IBF both rank him in their top 10. Under the Blotter's amateur-verification standard (see Rules Referenced), pedigree alone earns a flag, and the pro thesis is unproven.

Behind him sits a pipeline. Uzbekistan is Central Asian, not Eastern European, though its amateur system descends from Soviet-style programs. Under Part Thirteen, the Blotter's Fighter Origins Framework, it is treated as a closed system: results inside it say little about the world outside. The country won 5 of the 7 men's gold medals at Paris 2024 (TSS). Queensberry has signed Muydinkhujaev and Ummataliev, and that batch has been slow to rise (Bad Left Hook). The question for readers is whether the pipeline produces professionals or champions nobody has yet tested.

Rules Referenced

Rule 46 · Fighter Environment Standard. Every main-event and co-main fighter gets an assessment of five variables: trainer, promotional situation, management, broadcast platform and camp signals. It is a narrative flag system, not a checklist; only the variables live for this fight are written up.

Rule 34 and the amateur-verification standard. An amateur claim must be verified before it is used. Amateur pedigree alone earns a fighter a Flag. It does not earn a place among the proven prospects; the professional record has to do that.

Part Thirteen · Fighter Origins Framework. A lens for how a fighter's training background affects the matchup. Origin is context, not conclusion. A closed system is a state-backed national program with central coaching (the Uzbek pipeline is one), so results inside it say little about the world outside it.

FLAGGED. The Prospect Watch status for a fighter whose amateur pedigree is strong but whose professional evidence is not yet proven.

Rule 74 · Stoppage Rate. Knockouts divided by wins, with the method shown beside the percentage, for example 96% (22/23).

Rule 88 and UNVERIFIED. Co-main fighters get a Crisis Execution Rating just as the main event does, which grades how a fighter performs when hurt. When no tape has been reviewed and only records exist, the rating is marked UNVERIFIED rather than guessed.

Rule 48 · BoxRec Links. A fighter links to BoxRec only when the profile ID has been verified from the page itself. A wrong link is worse than no link.
