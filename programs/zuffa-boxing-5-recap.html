Cortes Record

Cortes came in ranked No. 9 by The Ring and No. 4 by the WBO at junior lightweight. He had never fought at 135. Garcia had beaten Taiga Imanaga in Riyadh in December, won six straight, and entered as the favorite. The setup was legitimate.

What Cortes showed over ten rounds was the ability to work inside consistently, accumulate, and keep Garcia from getting into a rhythm with his pressure. He out-landed Garcia 95 to 67 — a gap that held across the full ten rounds, not just one or two big sequences.

Garcia had a point deducted in the fourth round for holding. That affected the narrowest scorecard (95-94) more than it should have needed to, which is worth noting. Even without it, Cortes was ahead on all three cards. The 97-92 card was the most accurate reading of how the fight actually went.

Garcia's southpaw pressure never fully materialized. He had moments in the middle rounds where he walked Cortes to the ropes, but Cortes kept rotating out and staying busy. Garcia's one-loss came by KO1 — a warning about his chin in situations where he's not controlling distance — but Cortes didn't look for the stoppage here. He fought a volume fight and won it cleanly.

The question Cortes needed to answer: can he win at 135? He answered it. The next question is whether the Zuffa lightweight landscape produces a ranked opponent who tests him more than Garcia did. Magsayo is now the name at this weight on this roster.

Mark Magsayo won a WBC featherweight title in 2022 by stopping Gary Russell Jr. Moving to lightweight is a genuine test — two full weight classes is not cosmetic. His first fight at 135 needed to mean something. It did.

Magsayo rocked McCrory with right hands in the first round and never let him settle. The body work accumulated through the middle rounds, and by Round 5 McCrory's corner saw enough. The stoppage came 21 seconds in — the corner pulled him rather than letting the referee do it. That is the right call when the cumulative damage is already visible.

McCrory came in at 17-1 and had been a legitimate title contender. This was not a soft debut. Magsayo-Cortes is now the obvious fight on this roster.

Story of the Night
KO of the Year Candidate
De La Cerda
KO3 · Fuller III · 2:33 · Featherweight

Alexis De La Cerda came in as the underdog against Ervin Fuller III, who was 12-0 and the more experienced fighter. Fuller actually edged the first two rounds working behind a Philly Shell.

In the third, De La Cerda baited Fuller into a close-quarters exchange, drew him forward with the threat of a left hook — Fuller ducked under it — and as Fuller came up to throw his own punch, De La Cerda's right hook was already loaded and landing. Fuller was face-down before he registered what happened.

The footage went viral overnight. De La Cerda is 9-0, 22 years old, out of Federal Way, Washington. Amateur background not yet on file. He goes on the Next on Radar list immediately. Fuller's Philly Shell was working right up until it wasn't — that's the exact moment a fighter with De La Cerda's timing needs.

Next on Radar — formal entry pending amateur verification.

Upset of the Year −2500
Hirsch Jr.
MD8 · Meriwether III · 77-75, 78-74 · LW

Robert Meriwether III was the Performance of the Night winner on Zuffa Boxing 01. He entered as a -2500 favorite — that is not a misprint. At those odds, you are not expected to lose to Tony Hirsch Jr., who came in at 6-0-2.

Hirsch was the better, more consistent boxer for most of eight rounds. One judge had it even at 76-76. The other two gave it to Hirsch at 77-75 and 78-74. Meriwether dropped to 10-1 and has questions to answer about whether his Zuffa 01 performance was the ceiling or a preview.

Hirsch improves to 8-0-2. There is no system for scouting a 6-0-2 fighter who upsets a -2500 favorite on a national platform. He either found his level tonight or he exposed Meriwether's. Probably some of both.

Meriwether's development track now has a significant question mark attached.

Alexis De La Cerda
9-0 · 5 KOs · Featherweight · Federal Way, Washington · 22 years old

The knockout of Fuller III is not just a highlight. It shows a specific skill: the ability to set a trap in close quarters, load the right hand on a timed duck, and deliver it the moment the opponent commits. Fuller had a Philly Shell working. He was winning rounds with it. De La Cerda baited him into the exact position the shell is vulnerable to and pulled the trigger once.

The casual fan has never heard of him. He was the underdog on a prelim. That is the exact profile this column exists for.

B+
Vol. I · No. 5 · Zuffa Boxing 5 · April 5, 2026
The main event was clean and competitive but not explosive — Cortes answered the 135-pound question with a volume win, not a statement. That alone is a B card. The undercard is what lifts it. De La Cerda's KO3 of Fuller is a legitimate KO of the Year candidate. Hirsch upsetting a -2500 Meriwether is the biggest upset of the year on any platform. Two extraordinary individual moments on the same night, both from fighters who were not on the casual fan's radar coming in. Zuffa 5 over-delivered relative to what the main event alone would have earned.
