The Read
Two Southpaw Olympians The Division Forgot
The winner of tonight's fight steps directly into the conversation at 175 pounds. The loser has to start over. Neither fighter has been given a fair look at the top. That changes tonight.

This fight was supposed to happen in February. Ramirez went to the hospital with appendicitis and it was postponed. It was rescheduled. Now it is happening. The delay is worth noting because Richards was ready in February — committed to the fight, prepared, waiting — and had to absorb the disruption and rebuild momentum. That is a psychological variable that rarely gets documented. It is documented here.

Albert Ramirez is 22-0 with 19 knockouts. The 86% stoppage rate is not manufactured against soft opposition — he won the WBA interim title in Benghazi, Libya, stopping Jerome Pampellone in seven rounds in a foreign country on an unfamiliar platform. That is the Volunteer Profile. A fighter who accepts hard assignments when easier ones are available. He is The Ring's third-ranked light heavyweight. He is 33 years old, a 2016 Olympian from Venezuela who built a career in Montreal while the division's power brokers looked elsewhere. He has a four-inch reach advantage over Richards and a power differential that is not subtle — 86% versus 21%.

Lerrone Richards is 19-1 with 91 amateur wins and nine national titles. His only professional loss was a sixth-round TKO to Steed Woodall in June 2024 — a fight that temporarily derailed what was building to be a significant run. He has won four straight since. He has 131 professional rounds to Ramirez's 84. He has been in longer fights, harder fights, championship rounds at super middleweight. He moved up to light heavyweight to find bigger opportunities. Tonight he is the visiting challenger in Ramirez's promotional home.

The fight lives on one question: can Richards make it ugly enough long enough to offset the power? His jab, his movement, his 131 rounds of experience in championship-length fights — those are real tools. But Ramirez's left hand is a different instrument than anything Richards has absorbed at 175 pounds. The chin has not been tested at this level against this kind of power. That is the critical unknown.

Rule 46 — Fighter Environment
Eye of the Tiger Management promotes Ramirez and runs this card. Richards is the visiting challenger from Frank Warren's promotional stable. When both fighters are not under the same banner, the card's promoter controls the broadcast positioning, opponent selection, and post-fight narrative. Richards accepted those terms to get the fight. That is the structural reality of tonight's matchup — and it is worth knowing as you watch.
Undercard Files
Imam Khataev vs. Mickael Diallo
Co-Main  ·  NABF LHW Title  ·  10 Rds
Khataev is 11-1, 10 KOs — 90% stoppage rate. His only loss was a split decision to David Morrell, one of the best light heavyweights alive. Tokyo 2020 Olympian, 2023 World Championships bronze medal. He is the most decorated fighter on this undercard. Diallo is 21-2-2 with legitimate experience. But Khataev's power at this level is well-documented. Watch for the right hand — that is his primary finisher.
Dzmitry Asanau vs. Roger Gutierrez
IBF Intercontinental & WBC Continental Americas LW  ·  10 Rds
Asanau is 12-0 with an amateur résumé that belongs in a different conversation entirely — 200+ victories, three European Championship gold medals, two Olympic appearances (Rio 2016 and Tokyo 2021), amateur wins over Andy Cruz and Murodjon Akhmadaliev. At 29, his professional record doesn't yet reflect what the amateur pedigree implies. Tonight is a mandatory defense. Gutierrez is 29-7-1 with 22 KOs — experienced, dangerous, not a stepping stone. This is worth watching closely. See Blotter Crossover notation below.
Wyatt Sanford vs. Juan Pablo Ilharregui
8 Rounds  ·  Lightweights
Sanford is Canada's first Olympic boxing medalist in 28 years — Paris 2024 bronze, 2023 Pan American Games gold. He is 6-0 as a professional, fighting at home in Montreal in front of his adopted city. Ilharregui is the opponent. Sanford is the story. See Blotter Crossover notation below.
Jhon Orobio vs. Jonathan Montrel
WBC Continental Americas SLW Title  ·  10 Rds
Orobio is 17-0, 15 KOs — 88% KO rate at 22 years old. Colombian, based in Montreal, trained by Marc Ramsay. Pan American championship competitor and Junior World Championships bronze medalist as an amateur. Montrel is 19-4, 13 KOs — legitimate opposition with real experience. This is the most important undercard fight on the card for Prospect Watch purposes. See full entry below.

Jhon "El Tigre" Orobio
17–0  ·  15 KOs  ·  88% KO Rate  ·  Super Lightweight  ·  Age 22
Born in Cali, Colombia. Based in Montreal. Discovered boxing at ten years old through Muhammad Ali. Originally a soccer player. Trained by Marc Ramsay — the same trainer who works with Ramirez and Asanau. That is not a coincidence. Ramsay runs one of the best lightweight-to-welterweight development programs in North America, and Orobio was invited into it because the talent was undeniable.

Amateur Background
Represented the Colombian national team. Competed at the Pan American Championship and won bronze at the Junior World Championships. Brief amateur career by elite standards — 13 documented bouts — but the pedigree is real. He was in international competition before most fighters his age had turned professional.
Thesis
The 88% KO rate at 22 years old is not the thesis — it is the evidence. The thesis is the style: Ramsay-trained fighters start with the jab, use footwork to control range, and build combinations from the body up. Orobio has that foundation at an age when most fighters are still learning to throw in combinations. The WBC Continental Americas title defense tonight against Montrel — a 19-fight veteran with 13 KOs — is the first real test of whether the thesis holds against legitimate opposition.
Verification Threshold
How does he perform against Montrel's experience and power tonight? If he dominates — not just wins, but controls — this entry gets upgraded immediately. If he struggles, the thesis is under review.
