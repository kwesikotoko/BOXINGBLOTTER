Game Story
The Fight That Refused to Be Neat
Wilder won. The Blotter had Chisora. Both of those things are true and neither one fully explains what happened inside the O2 on Saturday night.

The Blotter called this fight for Chisora. The judges gave it to Wilder. That is the accountability statement and it goes at the top, without qualification. A miss is a miss.

What the Blotter got right: the fight was close. CBS Sports scored it 113-113 even. The one dissenting judge gave it to Chisora 112-115. The CompuBox data showed Chisora out-landing Wilder in power punches 105 to 99 and matching his 37% connect rate across twelve rounds. The analytical case for Chisora was not invented — it was built from real data and real tendencies. It was still wrong.

What the Blotter missed: Wilder won the rounds that mattered most to the judges. He controlled the early frames with his jab and straight right hand — more disciplined, more consistent than his recent outings had suggested. When Wilder sets range and times opponents on the way in, he is still capable of winning rounds cleanly. He did that in the first half of the fight.

The fight's central chapter was round eight. Wilder hurt Chisora, backed him into the corner with a sustained right hand barrage, sent him to the canvas, watched him beat the count, then pushed him through the ropes. Referee Mark Bates ruled the push a second knockdown — contested by most observers — then deducted a point from Wilder for the shove. The net result: two knockdowns, a point deduction, and a round of extraordinary complexity for the judges to interpret. Chisora, who had every reason to fold, came back with a right hand that wobbled Wilder before the bell.

The eleventh round matched the eighth for chaos. Wilder landed a right that sent Chisora through the ropes for a second knockdown. Seconds later Chisora caught Wilder with a looping right that sent him down — ruled a slip by Bates. Both fighters taking turns on the canvas with different official interpretations. The crowd on their feet not knowing who was winning or why.

After the final bell Wilder spoke about round eight and why he backed off after hurting Chisora badly. He said he saw Chisora's temple swelling and told him in the ring that he had to live for his kids. Whatever its sincerity, the Blotter notes this as analytically significant: the Danger Flag called Wilder mentally past this fight. What actually happened was more complicated than either dismissal or vindication. He was present enough to win. He was also present enough to choose mercy over finishing instinct. Both things happened in the same round.

Round by Round
Round Score Narrative
R1 10–9 W Wilder controls range with jab and right hand. Bizarre opening when a Chisora cornerman enters the ring during a clinch — warning issued, no penalty despite this being a disqualifiable offense under BBBofC rules. Both fighters warned for rabbit punches. Wilder's early discipline sets the tone.
R2 10–9 W Wilder snaps Chisora's head back with a sharp right uppercut and follows with a flurry in the corner. Clearest round of the fight. Chisora pressing but eating the straighter shots.
R3 10–9 W Wilder continues behind the jab. Chisora lands a solid right but Wilder times him going in. Eye trouble flagged for Chisora at the bell. Chisora complaining about something — Wilder attacks before referee intervenes.
R4 10–9 C Chisora finally finds his range with overhand rights that wobble Wilder late in the round. Wilder down from a wrestling clinch — no knockdown. Chisora's mass begins making life uncomfortable. Turning point in momentum.
R5 Even Messy. Both fighters trading clinches, neither landing with authority. Chisora working the body. Wilder looking for one big right. Scorecards likely split on this round across the three judges.
R6 10–9 W Wilder opens a cut over Chisora's left eye with a one-two in the final seconds. Blood begins to flow. Chisora's camp now has a medical problem to manage alongside the tactical one.
R7 10–9 C Chisora responds aggressively, pushing Wilder against the ropes and landing heavy overhand rights. Wilder beginning to look fatigued. The weight differential doing its work in close.
⚡ ROUND 8 — THE CHAOS ROUND · Wilder KD · Chisora KD · Point deducted from Wilder (push) · See The Moment above · Net: 9-8 Wilder on most cards, two knockdowns each scored differently
R9 10–9 W Wilder back behind the jab, landing clean right hands. Chisora pressing but punches losing snap. Body work from Chisora keeping him in the round but not winning it. Wilder controlling distance again.
R10 10–9 C Chisora pins Wilder in the corner early and lands several left hands. Wilder fires back but Chisora's big overhand right wobbles him. The crowd back in it. Championship rounds beginning to define the fight.
R11 Contested Wilder right hand sends Chisora through the ropes — ruled knockdown by Bates. Seconds later Chisora catches Wilder with a looping right, Wilder goes down — ruled a slip. Two falls, one knockdown, one slip, same round. CBS scored this a 10-10. Most scorers had Wilder ahead on the round.
R12 10–9 W Wilder controlling with jab in final frame. Chisora still pressing in what may be his final three minutes as a professional fighter. Both men refusing to yield. Standing ovation at the final bell. Wilder told Chisora he deliberately backed off — "looked after him" — in deference to Chisora's children. Chisora later said "If I didn't come out of the ropes, I would have won that fight."
"I saw his eyes swell and his temple start to bump. I said, 'Bro, you have to live for your kids.' Too many lives have been lost in this ring. Tonight, I looked out for him."

Player Verdicts
Exceeded Expectations
Deontay Wilder
More disciplined than his recent outings. Won the early rounds with a functioning jab — something the form decline flag suggested he might not have. Still dangerous enough to land the right hand that defined the fight. The voluntary backing-off in R8 is analytically complex: not the ruthless finisher of 2017-2019, but showing a kind of emotional self-possession that prime Wilder arguably lacked. He called out Joshua post-fight. The division has to take that seriously now.
Delivered
Derek Chisora
Out-landed Wilder. Matched his connect rate. Took two knockdowns and came back swinging both times. Is 42 years old and made a former WBC champion work every minute of all twelve rounds. Left the ring with his daughter beside him and refused to confirm retirement. "I'm going home with the boss lady and see." The man who showed up Saturday night deserved better than the verdict he received. Whether he earned it is the question 12 judges would answer 12 different ways.
Co-Feature Result — The Subplot Lives
WBO Interim Middleweight Title · 12 Rounds
Denzel Bentley def. Endry Saavedra — TKO Round 7
Bentley record: 22-3-1 · 18 KOs · Saavedra record: 17-2-1
The Blotter called Bentley TKO rounds 6-9. It happened in round 7. Clean hit. The more important story: the Janibek subplot the Blotter flagged in the program is now live. Bentley is the WBO interim middleweight champion and the mandatory challenger. Janibek Alimkhanuly — who beat Bentley in 2022 — is suspended by the WBO for one year after testing positive for meldonium. He remains the titlist on paper but cannot defend. If Janibek does not return to 160 pounds, the winner of tonight's fight inherited more than an interim title. Bentley positioned for a world title shot through the back door of a governing body's disciplinary action. The Blotter watches this fight, this fighter, and this division going forward. The program was right to flag the subplot. The result confirmed it has legs.
IBF World Title Eliminator · EBU European Cruiserweight Title · 12 Rounds
Viddal Riley def. Mateusz Masternak — Unanimous Decision
118-110 · 118-110 · 119-109 · Riley record: 14-0, 7 KOs
Called Riley UD. Got Riley UD. The analytical question — how does Riley handle a man with 50 fights of professional experience — was answered convincingly. He controlled behind his jab and footwork, outlanded Masternak consistently, and kept exchanges at his preferred range for eleven of the twelve rounds. Masternak had a strong final round but the lead was long since established. Riley is 28, unbeaten, and now the IBF mandatory challenger for the cruiserweight title. First real test passed. The Blotter notes it and moves on to the next question.

Lesson File — New Entries
Permanent Lesson File · April 4, 2026
Form decline over the last five fights is a real analytical tool — but it does not account for a fighter who changes trainer, changes style, and brings more disciplined fundamentals than his recent tape suggests. When a fighter makes a documented camp change, that must be weighted separately. The Wilder-Pedro Diaz note was in the research. It was not weighted enough in the call.
A fighter's power in the early rounds can win a fight on the scorecards even if the CompuBox data shows the opponent out-landing him across all 12. Wilder won rounds 1-3 cleanly. Those rounds are worth the same on the scorecard as any others. The Blotter overweighted Chisora's late-fight pressure and underweighted Wilder's early round control.
The "Danger Flag" — fighter mentally looking past the opponent — applies when the fighter's preparation and approach suggests disengagement. Wilder trained properly and engaged fully. The flag was applied based on his public comments about Usyk. Public statements are not reliable indicators of competitive focus once the bell rings.
Legacy
What Saturday Night Left Behind

Wilder called out Anthony Joshua after the fight. That matchup — two former heavyweight champions, both past their peak, both with something left to prove — is the most commercially logical fight available to either man. The Blotter will have a program when it is confirmed.

Chisora's farewell is unconfirmed. He walked into the O2 through the public entrance with his daughter. He walked out with his wife and told her the question mark was hers to answer. That is not the language of a man who has made peace with the ending. The Blotter does not call the retirement until Chisora does.

The night's most lasting image may not be a punch. It is Wilder telling a man he just knocked down that he has to live for his kids — and then choosing not to finish him. Whether that was mercy or competitive softness depends on which night of Wilder's career you are watching. On Saturday it was both, simultaneously, in the same round. That is the version of Deontay Wilder who won the 100th combined fight of his and Chisora's careers. Neither exactly what was expected nor exactly what was feared. Just alive, still standing, and already looking for the next one.
