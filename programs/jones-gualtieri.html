The Story
First Former Champion. First Real Test.

Golden Boy Promotions hasn't brought a card to the Bay Area in nearly two decades. Amari Jones is the reason they came back — 23 years old, 16–0, trained by Virgil Hunter, fighting in front of a crowd that watched him grow up.

Jones is also fighting a former world champion for the first time. Vincenzo Gualtieri won the IBF middleweight title by beating Esquiva Falcao in 2023, lost it to Janibek Alimkhanuly in six rounds, and has won four straight since. He came in ranked fifth in the IBF. There is something to find out Friday.

The co-main deserves equal attention. Robin Safar and Yamil Peralta are fighting for the WBC Silver cruiserweight title in a matchup most of the room hasn't tracked.

Main Event  ·  IBF Middleweight Eliminator  ·  12 Rounds
Fighter Profiles
Amari "The Reaper" Jones

Oakland, California. 23 years old. Pro debut April 2021. Multiple national championships as an amateur — documented per Golden Boy's confirmed record. Trained by Virgil Hunter, who called Jones "one of the best fighters I have trained since Andre Ward." He is a switch-hitter with height, power, and the patience Hunter builds into every fighter he develops. 14 knockouts in 16 fights — 87.5%. Most recent win: fourth-round stoppage of Luis Arias on the Barrios–Garcia card, February 2026. Ranked fourth in the IBF. He has never shared a ring with anyone who has held a world title.

Vincenzo "Il Capo" Gualtieri

Born in Germany, Italian roots, based in Munich. 33 years old. Pro debut 2016. Beat Esquiva Falcao to win the IBF middleweight title on July 1, 2023 — Falcao's first professional loss. Lost it to Janibek Alimkhanuly in round six three months later. That stoppage belongs in context: Gualtieri absorbed punishment for five rounds before the fight was ended. He wasn't demolished early. Won four straight in Germany since. 103 professional rounds. 8 knockouts in 26 fights — not a finisher, but his defense and ring intelligence are what got him a title.

Matchup Factors
The KO Rate Question Unverified
Jones's 14 knockouts in 16 fights reads as elite finishing ability. The opposition qualifies that number. He has never faced a former world champion, a ranked contender with defensive credentials, or anyone who fights like Gualtieri. Whether the rate holds at this level is what Friday answers. Gualtieri's one professional stoppage loss was to Janibek Alimkhanuly. Jones is not Janibek.
Championship Round Experience Gualtieri Edge
Gualtieri has 103 professional rounds. Jones has roughly 60. Gualtieri went six rounds with Janibek — absorbed world-class combinations and stayed upright for most of it. If this gets complicated in rounds 8 through 12, those extra 40 rounds count for something. Jones has not been tested past the early rounds by anyone capable of hurting him.
Virgil Hunter's System Jones Edge
Hunter's fighters are built for the long arc. Jones's amateur foundation plus Hunter's professional development puts him technically ahead of most 23-year-olds at this stage. His combination work, ability to switch stances, and jab-to-power-shot sequencing are all Hunter signatures. Whether that translates against a former world champion is a separate question from whether the system works.
Gualtieri's Durability Gualtieri Edge
30.8% knockout rate over 26 fights. Gualtieri is not a finisher — and the other side of that is he does not get stopped. He survived Janibek for five and a half rounds. Jones's power has not been tested against this level of defensive intelligence or this level of opponent. The question is whether Gualtieri can make Jones miss cleanly enough to neutralize the power advantage over 12 rounds.
The Call  ·  Jones vs. Gualtieri
Jones   UD
Unanimous Decision  ·  12 Rounds
Jones wins. Bigger, younger, more physically gifted, and trained by the best developer in the sport. The method is the call: he does not stop Gualtieri. Gualtieri survived Janibek for five and a half rounds. His defensive intelligence frustrates Jones through the middle rounds. Jones wins clean on the scorecards. Gualtieri is still standing at the final bell — and that's the piece of information this fight produces.

Co-Main Event  ·  WBC Silver Cruiserweight Title  ·  12 Rounds
Safar vs. Peralta  ·  Fight Analysis
Safar  vs  Peralta Watch This
WBC Silver Title Defense
Safar (19–0) · Peralta (18–1–1)

Robin Safar is undefeated, 19–0, based in Las Vegas, with a 68% finish rate. His résumé headline is a unanimous decision over Sergey Kovalev in Riyadh in May 2024. He is ranked WBC #12 and WBO #4.

Yamil Peralta is from Buenos Aires, 34 years old, a two-time Olympian per the WBC's documentation for this fight. He is ranked WBC #2 — 10 spots ahead of Safar in the same rankings. He holds the WBC Silver title that's being defended Friday. His one loss — a decision to Ryan Rozicki in Canada — was controversial enough that the rematch was declared a draw. This is his U.S. debut.

Safar's defining win came over a very aged Kovalev. Peralta is #2 in the rankings Safar is #12 in. The gap between those two numbers is the pick.

Undercard Watch  ·  Fulghum vs. Urrutia
Fulghum vs. Urrutia
Super Middleweights  ·  10 Rounds
Fulghum (14–1–1) · Urrutia (17–2)

Darius "DFG" Fulghum holds the most interesting résumé on this undercard. 2018 National Golden Gloves champion at 201 pounds — documented on BoxRec. Lost a unanimous decision to Bektemir Melikuziev in a WBA eliminator. Drew with David Stevens. Now 29, he changed camps for this cycle — working in Los Angeles with Julian Chua alongside trainer Darnell Pierce.

Yoanki Urrutia is a Cuban system product, 17–2, based in Miami. His most recent loss was to Ryan Adams, who carries a 12–11–1 record. The question Friday is not whether Fulghum wins. It's whether he looks sharp enough to stay in the world title conversation.

The Call
Fulghum   TKO Rd. 4
Fulghum's power ends it before the midpoint. A win here doesn't move the needle — how he wins does. Looking flat against a fighter who just lost to a .500 opponent would be notable.
