The Blotter called Edwards with HIGH confidence. The result was not close. Nqothole was rangy, relaxed, and in total control for twelve rounds. His pace did not slow. He countered while pressing. At times he made Edwards look as if he did not belong in the ring. That is how badly the call missed — not a split decision that went the wrong way, but a performance that exposed the call as analytically indefensible.

How it happened: the program built a three-act story around Edwards — the fall, the rebuild, the second chance. That architecture was real. The error was letting the story drive the call. The physical data was in the research — Nqothole holds a 3.5-inch reach advantage at 115 pounds, has never been stopped in 24 professional fights, and entered on a three-fight win streak against ranked opposition. Those facts were written down in the program. The call went the other way regardless. That is a discipline failure, not a research failure.

The Blotter's reputation is built on what the call record shows over time. A miss this glaringly wrong — HIGH confidence on the wrong fighter, against a man who made the opponent look outclassed — is exactly the kind of call that erodes credibility if it becomes a pattern. It will not become a pattern. The lesson is filed. The record reflects it accurately and permanently.

PERMANENT LESSON FILED — Edwards-Nqothole, May 29, 2026: Narrative architecture is not a substitute for physical profile analysis. When rankings, reach, height, and fight history point one direction and the story points another, the data wins. HIGH confidence requires physical match parity. It does not require a compelling backstory.
Main Event
Nqothole Made It Look Like the Rankings Were Right All Along
He established the jab in round one and never gave it up. Edwards spent twelve rounds trying to solve a geometry problem that the tape had already told the Blotter was unsolvable.
Nqothole lands clean on Edwards · York Hall · Photo: MF Pro / DAZN · May 29, 2026

Nqothole was rangy, relaxed, and in total control from round one. He established his jab and never surrendered it — not under pressure, not when the York Hall crowd got loud. His pace did not slow. He pressed the action while countering simultaneously, landing clean and stepping back before Edwards could answer. At times he made Edwards look as if he did not belong in the ring.

Edwards found moments — he is a former world champion and the foundation is real. But the 117-111 card credited him with two rounds. The 116-113 card gave him three. Neither reading was wrong. Nqothole traveled to Bethnal Green, fought in a hostile room, and never changed his game plan. The Blotter called the other man. The tape called Nqothole.

Active Watch · Sikho Nqothole
Blotter Active Watch · Post-Fight Filing
Sikho "Sequence" Nqothole
22-3 · 13 KOs · IBF Mandatory Challenger · Super Flyweight · Mthatha, South Africa · 31 years old

The Blotter underweighted this fighter in the pre-fight program and filed a call that was wrong. This column corrects that by filing a full analytical note now that the performance is documented.

A 69.5-inch reach at 115 pounds is a style problem for virtually every fighter in the super flyweight division. The fighters who compete at 115 are built to handle compact, shorter opponents — quick, physical, inside fighters. Nqothole turns every fight into a distance management problem. The opponent has to close seven inches of height and four inches of reach against a fighter who knows exactly how to use both.

Your read on the Bam Rodriguez matchup is worth documenting. If Rodriguez stays at 118 or above, the IBF super flyweight picture opens. Nqothole is now the mandatory. Garcia defends on June 6. If Nqothole wins that fight — and his physical profile gives him a real case — he becomes the IBF champion. A Bam return to 115 against a rangy southpaw with a 69.5-inch reach is a genuinely difficult stylistic assignment, one that the casual fan has not been asked to consider yet. The Blotter has asked it. The answer is not obvious in Bam's favor.

Nqothole was denied his 2016 Rio Olympic shot when SASCOC overturned his qualifying result and sent him to a secondary qualifier in Baku, where he failed to advance. He turned professional in 2017. Nine years of professional development built on a foundation the public was never told about. This column is telling it now.

Undercard
Toward vs. Corrie — Split Draw 96-94 / 94-96 / 95-95 · WBA Continental JMW
The Blotter called Corrie by decision. The result was a split draw — which means one judge had Toward winning, one had Corrie, and one had it even. The pre-fight note was correct: both fighters were more concerned with not losing than winning. The draw is the honest result of two fighters who fought nervously. Nothing settled, rematch likely. The Blotter confidence was LOW on this call — that rating was correct for the wrong reason. Filed as a draw, not a miss.
Cooper def. Osborne — TKO R8 2:58 · English Super Middleweight Title · Cooper 13-0
The Blotter called Cooper by decision. He won by TKO in the eighth round — a better result than called. Osborne had the conditioning of a fighter built for three-round tournament formats; the fatigue showed from round six onward. Cooper landed a second knockdown late in round eight and the fight was stopped. His York Hall debut produced the English title. Call result: correct winner, wrong method — but the direction was right and the manner reflects the size and conditioning gap the pre-fight program documented. Cooper is now 13-0 with the English title. The British title is the logical next step.
Welland def. Kassim — UD 99-91 / 98-92 / 99-91 · Commonwealth Youth FW Title
Welland wins the Commonwealth Youth featherweight title convincingly. The Blotter called him correctly — and the record flag from the pre-fight stands: Welland entered 8-1, having been stopped by Herrera on the Wilder-Chisora undercard seven weeks earlier. He answered that question with a dominant performance. The amateur foundation held. The scores (99-91 on two cards) say this was not close. Welland is 11-1 and has a title. The Herrera loss looks more like a step-up-too-soon than a ceiling indicator.
Roehrig def. Tolaj — TKO R2 Cruiserweight · No call filed
The Blotter filed no call on this fight due to insufficient documentation. Roehrig wins by TKO in two rounds, improving to 9-0 with 5 KOs. The no-call was the correct decision — the result is noted, no analytical position was staked.
