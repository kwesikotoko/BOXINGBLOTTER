Game Story
What Happened
Jones Outgrows the Label in Under Nine Minutes
Round 1  ·  Jones establishing the jab that set up everything that followed  ·  SAP Center, San Jose Cris Esqueda / Golden Boy

That jab was the whole fight in a single image. Jones used it in round one to measure Gualtieri, used it in round two to build the body work, and used it in round three to set up the right hand combination that put Gualtieri on the canvas for the first time. Then the second knockdown followed, and the fight was waved off at 2:29.

Jones controlled the opening two rounds methodically — no drama, no early exchanges, just accumulation. When the finish came in round three it wasn't a sudden explosion. It was the conclusion of work that had been building since the opening bell. Oscar De La Hoya at ringside: "I saw maturity, calmness and patience — and yet aggression. That was an absolute masterstroke."

Gualtieri came to California to recapture the IBF title he won against Esquiva Falcao in 2023. He ran into a fighter who has outgrown the prospect label. The first stoppage loss of his professional career. At 33, the path back to a world title fight is considerably longer now.

The Moment
What the Program Got Wrong — and What It Means

The pre-fight program's signature call was that Jones does not stop Gualtieri. The logic was defensible — Gualtieri had survived Janibek Alimkhanuly for five and a half rounds, and Jones's power hadn't been tested against this level of opposition. The logic was wrong.

Jones used the jab and a body attack — exactly the Hunter signatures the program identified — to set up a right hand that Gualtieri couldn't adjust to. This wasn't a wild punch landing against the odds. It was the execution of a game plan that built correctly from round one through round three. The power translated. The ceiling this entry had been approaching is now confirmed.

The IBF title fight against the winner of Oliha-McKenna is the right next step. Jones earned it tonight.

Co-Main Event  ·  WBC Silver Cruiserweight Title
Safar vs. Peralta  ·  Split Decision
The moment before the scorecards — Safar (left) and Peralta (right) waiting on a split decision  ·  SAP Center Cris Esqueda / Golden Boy

Safar won by split decision. Scores: 116–111, 112–115, 114–113. Our pre-fight call was Peralta by majority decision. We called the wrong winner. What the scorecards don't fully reflect: Peralta put Safar on the canvas in round two. One judge gave the fight to Peralta by three rounds. The 116–111 card for Safar is the one the Blotter files a question mark against.

Scores: 116–111 Safar  ·  112–115 Peralta  ·  114–113 Safar  ·  Split Decision
First-Hand Scout's Report  ·  Yamil Peralta  ·  May 22, 2026
Peralta fought off the back foot setting traps throughout. Made clear adjustments mid-fight. He moved side to side and switched from conventional to southpaw — those angles disrupted Safar's timing and allowed Peralta to recalibrate his own. The key sequence: Peralta baited Safar, who was the pressure fighter throughout, by inviting him in and then beating him to the punch. Made Safar reset repeatedly. Crafty. The kind of ring intelligence that comes from Olympic competition — you don't learn that in four years of professional matchmaking.

Peralta knocked the undefeated fighter down in round two, won one judge's card by three rounds, and fought the more complete fight by the room's assessment. He lost the scorecards. The 116–111 card — which would have required Safar to win ten of twelve rounds against a fighter who put him on the canvas — is the number that doesn't sit right.

Blotter Watch  ·  Active Flag
Yamil Peralta  ·  Keep a Close Eye
He outfought an undefeated WBC-ranked contender in that man's promotional ecosystem and lost on two of three scorecards. His tactical profile — trap-setting off the back foot, mid-fight stance switch, baiting a pressure fighter and beating him to the punch — is not a style you see at this level without serious amateur foundation behind it. The next fight determines whether Peralta gets a formal Blotter entry or a program of his own.
Undercard  ·  Fulghum vs. Urrutia

Called Fulghum TKO round four. Urrutia did not come out for round four — corner stoppage after a knockdown in round three. One round early, same method, same winner. Fulghum is 15–1–1 with 13 knockouts. The opposition level needs to climb. A fighter with his background and a WBA eliminator loss to Melikuziev on his record cannot build a case on wins over opponents who lose to .500 fighters.
