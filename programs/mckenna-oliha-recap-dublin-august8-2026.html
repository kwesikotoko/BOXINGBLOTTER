McKenna Boxes His Way to the IBF Belt
Aaron McKenna, 27, of Monaghan beat Etinosa Oliha, 28, of Italy over 12 rounds for the vacant IBF middleweight title, in his first professional fight on Irish soil.

A world-title fight in Dublin has a sound to it, and for twelve rounds it belonged to Aaron McKenna. He walked into 3Arena as the third-ranked contender, the home favorite and the taller man by two inches, and he spent the night keeping Oliha exactly where he wanted him: at the far end of the jab and the left hook.

Oliha came in unbeaten at 22-0, ranked a spot above McKenna and ten and a half months out of the ring. He was never shut out of the fight, and the cards give him two, three and four rounds. But McKenna kept walking forward behind the jab, and by the middle of the fight what Oliha threw back was being smothered before it could build into anything.

There was no late drama because McKenna did not leave room for any. The final bell brought the scores, 118-110, 117-111 and 116-112, and with them a place in the record book: the first Irish middleweight world champion since Andy Lee, and the first Zuffa Boxing champion under one of the four major sanctioning bodies. The belt had been vacant since the IBF stripped Janibek Alimkhanuly in March after a failed drug test. It left the building with a Monaghan man.

Aaron McKenna
A champion with the whole division in front of him

McKenna said before the fight that he wants to unify the division and later move up as a multi-weight champion. A unification with WBO champion Denzel Bentley was agreed in early September, then blocked when the WBO ordered Bentley to face its mandatory challenger, Yoenli Hernandez. The IBF’s mandatory challenger is Amari Jones.

Sam Hickey
The distance question has an answer

Hickey, 6-0 with 3 KOs, was the Blotter’s Scout’s Chair pick on this card. He went into the seventh against a decorated opponent and finished it at 0:27. What comes next is a question of opposition, not stamina.
