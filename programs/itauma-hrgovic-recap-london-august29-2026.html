Game Story
⚑ Blotter Flag · Narrative Spine
The program called Itauma to stop Hrgovic in round nine. The round and the stoppage were right. The man who got stopped was not.
Eight Rounds of Promise, One Round of Truth
Moses Itauma, 21, landed the cleaner shots for most of a heavyweight title fight and had never been past round six. Filip Hrgovic, 34, waited him out and became the first Croatian heavyweight champion.

The O2 was loud for Itauma early, and he gave it a reason. His speed and his accuracy were a problem for Hrgovic from the opening bell, and he went to the body beautifully: 51 body shots landed over the fight to Hrgovic’s 16, all but one of them power punches. In round two a right hook put Hrgovic on the canvas. It was a quick one, more a stumble than a hurt, but a knockdown is a knockdown.

Itauma lands the right hand early · The O2 Arena, London

Then the fight changed shape, and you could see it before any number caught up. Around round three Itauma looked to be breathing heavier. Between rounds four and five he began to slow. By six Hrgovic was landing, and landing more. Itauma still had moments, and round eight was his biggest of the night, 26 landed from 65 thrown. Only three of those were jabs. That is a fighter reaching for a finish, not setting one up.

Itauma had never been past round six.Fourteen fights, one wall

Round nine was a different fight. Hrgovic landed 29 of 64, 28 of them power shots, and Itauma, pinned against the ropes with nothing left to answer with, took it. Howard Foster waved it off at 2:27 and the towel came in from Itauma’s corner right behind him. Afterward Hrgovic said it plainly: he was outboxed, he was losing every round, and the chin matters.

Itauma hit a brick wall. It seems he was pushed too fast, and with only six rounds of experience as his ceiling, it showed. Whether the cause is conditioning, experience or both is the question boxing fans are asking, and it is what the Blotter will be watching from here on out. He has a lot of talent. He is not ready to be mentioned with the heavyweight elites, and this performance was very disappointing. He stays on the Crossover tier of Grind and Climb and is not dropped to the tier below.

Berinchyk (left) and Noakes (right) trade in close · The O2 Arena, London
Sam Noakes (19-1 · 16 KOs) vs. Denys Berinchyk (19-2 · 9 KOs)
Noakes UD 10
Called: Noakes by TKO in the second half. Right man, wrong finish. The cards read 100-90, 99-91 and 98-92, and the Blotter had it 100-90 as well. Noakes took the vacant WBO International lightweight title through ring generalship, effective aggression, defense and cleaner punches. Berinchyk has now lost back to back.
Constantin Ursu (16-0 · 7 KOs) vs. Ben Vaughan
Ursu TKO 8
Called: Ursu by decision. Right man, wrong finish: a cut over Vaughan’s eye ended it on the doctor’s advice, and Ursu kept his British and Commonwealth welterweight titles.
Guido Vianello vs. Moses Jolly (12-0-1 · 8 KOs)
Draw, 76-76
Called: Vianello by decision or mid-rounds stoppage. A draw has no winner to grade, so it counts as neither a hit nor a miss. Jolly, the late addition on about two weeks’ notice, kept his unbeaten record against a ranked veteran.
Aloys Youmbi (12-1 · 9 KOs) vs. Sherif Musah
Youmbi decision
Called: Youmbi over Mike Perez. Perez withdrew and Musah stepped in, so the Call is void under Rule 60. It is not counted as a hit or a miss.
Nelson Hysa vs. Thomas Narmo (14-1 · 14 KOs)
Hysa stoppage
No Call. This WBO Global heavyweight title bout was not in the program at all, a gap in the full-card scan (Rule 49.3a). Hysa, unbeaten, stopped Narmo inside two rounds with an overhand right; Narmo had won all 14 of his fights by knockout coming in.

Two printed facts were simply wrong and are named below. Facts like those are not supposed to get past us, and they are our errors, not the fight’s.
Corrections to the program (Rule 94). The program said Hrgovic had never been put on the canvas in twenty-one fights; Itauma put him down in round two. It listed the co-main as twelve rounds; it was ten. It called the cruiserweight bout as Youmbi against Mike Perez; Perez withdrew and Youmbi fought Sherif Musah. It left the Hysa–Narmo bout off the card. And it shipped with the volume number, two photo credits and the undercard start time still unfilled; this is Volume 34.
Rules Referenced

The Blotter Mark and the Proofline (Rule 113). Every Call sits on a nine-slot line, our pick on the left and his opponent on the right. The shaded slots are our mark, and the recap puts a second marker where the ring landed. The distance between the two is counted in steps, and the closer the marks, the better the Call.

Late substitution (Rule 60). When a fighter named in a Call withdraws or is replaced, the Call is void. It is not counted as a hit or a miss.

Corrections to the program (Rule 94). When the recap finds a fact the program printed wrong, it names the error and the fix here instead of quietly editing the shipped program.

Full-card scan (Rule 49.3a). The Blotter checks every bout on the card, not only the headliners, for fighters worth tracking.

Filip Hrgovic
The First Croatian Heavyweight Champion Has a Window

Hrgovic says he wants unification, with Daniel Dubois and Agit Kabayel the names he keeps coming back to. The IBF’s mandatory challenger, Frank Sanchez, has taken a step-aside fee, which clears the road.

Moses Itauma
Not Finished, Not Ready

He left the ring on a stretcher, went to the hospital as a precaution, was home the next day, and says he will fight on. Until he is tested past round six again, there is no honest answer to whether he was rushed or can fix it. The talent is not in question.

Sam Noakes · Denys Berinchyk
A Statement Win and a Second Straight Defeat

Noakes now holds a belt and a lopsided win to go with it. Berinchyk, a former WBO lightweight champion, has lost twice in a row, and the Davis knockout now has company.
