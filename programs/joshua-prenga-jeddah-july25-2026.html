The Read — Main Event
⚑ Blotter Flag · Narrative Spine
This is billed as a tune-up for a Fury fight nobody has signed yet, but the actual question in the ring has nothing to do with Wembley. Ten months ago Joshua was counted out on his feet, dropped four times by a live puncher. Ten days after his most recent win, he survived a car crash that killed two of his own team. Prenga has never shared a ring with anyone who hits like that — and neither, really, has the version of Joshua walking out Saturday.
The Chin Question Nobody Can Answer Yet
Joshua is favored to walk through Kristian Prenga on name value, size, and championship pedigree alone. The actual fight is whether the durability that failed against Daniel Dubois in September 2024 has come back, or whether Jake Paul in December simply never tested it.

Joshua enters at 29-4 with 26 KOs, a two-time unified heavyweight champion whose last twelve months have carried more outside the ropes than in them. He stopped Jake Paul in six rounds in December, dropping the YouTuber-turned-boxer twice before breaking his jaw — an emphatic result against limited opposition. Ten days later, Joshua was a passenger in a car crash near Lagos, Nigeria that killed two members of his team, Sina Ghami and Latif "Latz" Ayodele, and left Joshua himself recovering from injury. This fight is his first appearance since. The last time Joshua shared a ring with a real heavyweight, Daniel Dubois knocked him down four times before the referee waved it off in round five — a title challenge that ended as clearly as any laid-glove result the division has seen. That result sits directly behind whatever happens against Prenga, whether Joshua's team acknowledges it publicly or not.

Prenga is 35, from Orosh, Albania, fighting out of Edgewater, New Jersey, and 20-1 with 20 knockouts — every win of his career has ended inside the distance. That number is genuinely rare, but the opposition behind it is the honest caveat: his best win, a fifth-round stoppage of gatekeeper Joey Dawejko, ended on a bicep-injury complaint rather than a clean finish, and his lone loss came by decision to Dutch journeyman Giovanni Auriemma in 2017, a fight that included a point deduction against him. Prenga has been scheduled past ten rounds only three times in his career and has never boxed the full twelve-round championship distance. This is, by a wide margin, the best fighter he has ever faced.

Physically this is close to even on paper — Joshua's 6'6" and 82-inch reach against Prenga's 6'5" frame — which strips away the size mismatch that usually explains a heavy favorite in fights like this. Both men are orthodox. What separates them isn't the tale of the tape; it's championship-level experience against a man who has never had to solve someone this complete.

The Blotter's watch condition is simple: does Prenga's pressure find Joshua's chin early, the way Dubois's right hand did, or does Joshua's jab and footwork keep this at the safe range where his class takes over? Prenga has never had to sustain output past round five against anyone who hit back. If this goes past the midpoint clean, the fight becomes about whether Joshua's engine and composure — both legitimate questions after the layoff — hold up longer than Prenga's chin does.

Blotter Call · Main Event · LEAN
Anthony Joshua — KO/TKO
Class and experience should tell against a 20-1 record built almost entirely on club-level opposition. The lean, not a stronger call, comes from the live Dubois-loss chin question — if Prenga's power finds him early and clean, this changes fast.

The Blotter Spotlight — WBO Super Middleweight Title
⚡ Rule 62 — Title Status and Sanctioning Framing
Sheeraz's First Defense
WBO Super Middleweight · Voluntary Defense · 63 Days as Champion Entering Fight Night

Sheeraz (23-0-1, 19 KOs) won the then-vacant WBO title with a second-round stoppage of Alem Begic in May, after Terence Crawford was stripped for failing to pay sanctioning fees. WBO president Gustavo Olivieri confirmed this is a permitted voluntary defense — Zachenhuber clears the minimum world-rating criteria as the WBO's #7, but this is not the mandatory obligation Sheeraz still owes inside his 180-day window.

That distinction is the actual story here. Sheeraz has publicly acknowledged that a win puts him in line for a mandatory defense against Jacob Bank — also on tonight's card — or a possible unification bout. The backlash calling this a "safe" voluntary before a Canelo Alvarez fight is real and on the record; Sheeraz's own response has been that staying active shouldn't require taking a 50/50 every time out.

Zachenhuber (29-1, 18 KOs) arrives off a loss, not a win — dropped a six-round decision to Pawel August (also on this card) three months ago before a fast bounce-back stoppage of Armin Ajrulai. A voluntary title challenger coming off a defeat is itself worth naming plainly rather than letting the record alone speak.

Kelly's First Defense
IBF Super Welterweight (154 lbs) · Voluntary Defense

Kelly (18-1-1, 9 KOs) won the title with a majority-decision upset of Bakhram Murtazaliev in January — both men hit the canvas in that fight, Murtazaliev in round four, Kelly in round nine. This is Kelly's first defense, and like Sheeraz's, it is a voluntary selection, not the mandatory. Agyarko's actual mandatory-eliminator path collapsed twice: first when Brandon Adams collapsed pre-weigh-in, then when the IBF's ordered eliminator against former champion Murtazaliev was declined by Agyarko's team, clearing the way for Kelly to select him voluntarily instead.

Agyarko (18-0, 7 KOs) is unbeaten and gets his first world title opportunity here regardless of the technical path that got him here. Eddie Hearn is already talking a Sebastian Fundora unification for Kelly if he wins — worth naming as the forward-looking stake, not the frame for tonight's fight itself.

Rule 46 · Fighter Environment
Environment Analysis · Jeddah Superdome
Anthony Joshua: Neutral-site venue, but far from unfamiliar territory — this is Joshua's fourth fight in Saudi Arabia and second at a Jeddah venue specifically (he lost the Usyk rematch at King Abdullah Sports City in this same city in 2022). Riyadh Season money and crowd support skew toward the bigger name regardless of nationality; expect a pro-Joshua atmosphere despite the neutral location.

Kristian Prenga: First appearance at this stage, this scale, and this distance from his usual East Coast base entirely. Prenga has built his career and fanbase on being a hometown draw for the Albanian diaspora in New Jersey — none of that home-crowd energy travels with him to Jeddah. First real test of composure in a genuinely hostile, unfamiliar room.
