The Blotter's own call flipped from Ruiz to Knyba in the final hours before the bell on layoff risk alone — and the layoff was the entire fight, not just a contributing factor.
The Jab That Never Stopped
Damian Knyba (18-1, 11 KOs), 30, used his 86-inch reach to out-jab and out-work a 36-year-old Andy Ruiz Jr. (35-3-1, 22 KOs) across twelve rounds, dropping him in the seventh in a comeback that never found its rhythm.

Knyba controlled the fight from the opening bell almost entirely on volume and length. He threw 848 punches to Ruiz's 408 and landed 191 to Ruiz's 119, and the jab alone did more work than Ruiz's entire output — 127 landed of 720 thrown, against Ruiz's 59 of 260. Ruiz was competitive in flashes, edging rounds 1, 3, and 9 on connects and tying 4 and 5, and his power-punch accuracy actually finished close to even (60 of 148 to Knyba's 64 of 128), with a clear body-work edge (26 landed to Knyba's 5). But he never threw more than 43 punches in a single round, and a fighter who built his career on volume and hand speed spent most of the night doing neither.

The turning point arrived in round seven. Knyba landed 35 total punches in the round, 25 of them power shots on 32 thrown — a 78% clip — capped by a heavy combination that put Ruiz on the canvas. Ruiz beat the count and made it to the bell, and the referee warned him in round eight that another bad moment would end the fight there. He answered with his best stretch of the night, rallying in rounds eight and nine and opening a cut over Knyba's left eye, but Knyba reclaimed the jab by round ten and closed the show, out-landing Ruiz 22-9, 18-10, and 19-8 across the final three rounds.

The judges never fully caught up to how one-sided the fight actually was — 117-110, 114-113, and 114-113, with both 114-113 cards drawing criticism from ringside media given the connect gap and the knockdown. TNT studio analysts Terence Crawford and Shawn Porter both had Knyba winning comfortably; Timothy Bradley Jr., the one voice who'd picked Ruiz beforehand, said afterward that if Ruiz couldn't beat this version of Knyba, there was no point in coming back for another run.

Andy Ruiz Jr. & Damian Knyba
A Comeback Answered With a Question Mark

Ruiz leaves Newark 35-3-1 with two former champions in the TNT studio suggesting he hang it up, and only one voice — Bradley, who picked him beforehand — holding out hope. The two-year layoff is now a confirmed problem, not a theoretical one, and whatever comes next for Ruiz has to answer that directly rather than lean on the 2019 Joshua win to carry the narrative.

For Knyba, this is the best win of his career sandwiched between two very different data points — the TKO loss to newly-crowned WBC champion Agit Kabayel in January, and now a signature victory over a former unified titleholder. The jab-and-reach blueprint worked for twelve full rounds against a spent Ruiz; whether it holds up against a heavyweight who isn't fighting off a layoff is the next real test.

Vito Mielnicki Jr.
The Résumé Finally Has a Real Name On It

Mielnicki's earlier schedule let him develop without ever answering where he stood among ranked middleweights — Williams, who'd already gone the distance with Carlos Adames, was supposed to be that answer, and Mielnicki made it one-sided. He's calling for Erislandy Lara next, a fighter who has barely fought in four years; expect the Blotter to track whether that fight actually gets made or whether a more active option takes its place.

Zaquin Moses
Home State, First Real Test, Passed Early

Moses's amateur pedigree and Matchroom signing were always going to mean more once he faced someone who could actually push him — Contreras, who'd taken three legitimate prospects the distance, was that opponent, and Moses didn't need the scheduled eight rounds to get through him. This is the kind of result that should move him from Prospect Watch monitoring toward a formal entry next volume.

Alan Chaves & Eridson Garcia
The Zero Is Gone, and So Is the Belt

Chaves came in 22-0 with a five-fight stoppage streak and left having been dropped four times, losing his undefeated record and the WBO Latino strap in the same night — the shock result of the card by every outlet's own account. Garcia's counterpunching and volume did what the pre-fight read flagged as a risk but didn't weight heavily enough. Where Chaves goes from his first loss, and whether Garcia gets a bigger platform off this, are both threads worth tracking.
