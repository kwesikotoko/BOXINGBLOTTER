Game Story
⚑ Blotter Flag · Narrative Spine
The Blotter called Berlanga to win by unanimous decision on the theory he was the steadier operator at 168 — instead he hit the deck in round two, and it took a comeback, not a boxing clinic, to get him to the same result by stoppage in round seven.
Berlanga Answers the Chin Question About Himself, Not Just Butler
A two-fight losing streak, one of them a first-ever stoppage, needed a clean rebound — instead Berlanga got dropped again before his power finally broke Steven Butler down in the seventh.

Berlanga opened patient, working behind the jab and keeping the fight at range exactly as the pre-fight Read flagged as the condition for the pick to hold. It didn't last. In the closing seconds of round two, a partially blocked Butler right hand — one that didn't even land clean — put Berlanga on the canvas for the first time since the Sheeraz fight a year earlier. He beat the count and made it to his corner, but the chin question the program raised about Butler's opposition had, in that instant, become a live question about Berlanga instead.

The turning point came fast. Berlanga abandoned the boxing plan in round three and went to the power game that's always been his identity, hurting Butler with a straight right early and pounding him for the rest of the round — Butler even resorted to an MMA-style takedown attempt trying to survive the pressure. Berlanga kept the heavier, cleaner work through rounds four and five, and all three judges had him ahead 57-55, 58-54, and 57-55 through six. He closed round six with a barrage of right hands that put Butler down for a 10-8 round.

Butler answered with one more big right hand to start round seven, showing the same durability that had carried him through four straight wins — but it was the last real resistance he offered. Berlanga landed a pair of left hooks that wobbled him badly, and referee Eric Dali stepped in at 1:14 to wave it off, then accidentally tackled Butler into the ropes on the way to stopping it. Berlanga cocked his right hand and stood over a downed Butler before showing mercy. "It's a learning experience, and it just shows that I'm a true champion," he said after.

The Moment
Round 6 · Closing Seconds
Berlanga, still fighting off the memory of the round-two knockdown, lands a full combination of right hands that finally buckles Butler and puts him on a knee in the center of the ring — the moment the fight stopped being competitive and started being a countdown.

Edgar Berlanga
The Chin Question Is Answered — Now It's About the Names

Getting off the floor to finish a durable opponent settles the immediate concern coming out of the Sheeraz knockdown streak. Berlanga called out Jaime Munguia, Jermall Charlo, Chris Eubank Jr., Caleb Plant, and a Sheeraz rematch after the fight — the next step is whether Zuffa Boxing can actually land one of those names rather than another opportunity-level opponent.

Richardson Hitchins
Welterweight Debut Answered Every Question It Could

A 100-90 shutout in a naturally bigger weight class is about as clean an argument as a first fight at 147 can make. He's already ranked WBA-5 and IBF-3 there; the matchmaking calculus his camp described pre-fight — a calculated step before a title shot within 12 months — looks more credible today than it did going in.

Sergiy Derevyanchenko
The Résumé Still Reads Elite. The Recent Record Doesn't.

He's lost seven of his last eleven going back to 2018 and turns 41 in October, but he stayed competitive enough to stagger the younger, faster Hackett in the tenth. The gap between what his name still means and what his recent form actually is keeps widening.
