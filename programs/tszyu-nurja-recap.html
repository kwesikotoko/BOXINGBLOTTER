Main Event
The Win Tszyu Needed. Not the Statement He Wanted.
The Blotter called TKO, rounds 5–9. Tszyu won every round on all three cards. Nurja — who had never been past six rounds — survived all ten. That is worth documenting.
Photo: No Limit Boxing · Tszyu stands over Nurja following the R4 knockdown · WIN Entertainment Centre · April 5, 2026

Tim Tszyu knocked Denis Nurja down in the fourth round and then spent six more rounds not finishing him. Nurja was deducted a point in the seventh for excessive holding — a fighter in survival mode doing what survival mode looks like. He made it to the final bell. He had never been past six rounds before tonight.

That is the gap between what happened and what the Blotter called. The result was correct — Tszyu was ahead on every card, every round, 100-88 three times. The manner is the question. A fighter with genuine finishing instinct, against a man with no championship-level experience, finds a way to end it when he has him on the floor in the fourth.

Tszyu said afterward he was trying to take Nurja out. The performance said something different. He absorbed a cut below his left eye early from a clash of heads and may have become cautious after that. Or the finishing gear that once made him dangerous at junior middleweight is still being reassembled under Pedro Diaz in Miami. Both things can be true at once.

What the pre-fight program asked: how does Tszyu respond if Nurja catches him clean? He got cut, kept working, and dominated. That part of the question he answered cleanly. What it didn't reveal is whether the two Fundora losses and the Murtazaliev stoppage have changed what happens when Tszyu has a man in serious trouble. He didn't test that gear tonight.

Errol Spence Jr. is the name Tszyu called out post-fight. Spence has not fought since Crawford stopped him in July 2023. If that fight gets made in the summer, Tszyu will need every round of experience he can get — including the ability to close fights when they are there to be closed. Tonight he left a question unanswered that Spence will be watching closely.

The Blotter Called · Pre-Fight vs. Result
Pre-fight call: Tszyu TKO, rounds 5–9. Actual result: Tszyu UD10, 100-88 × 3. Correct winner. Wrong method. Nurja — who had never been past six rounds professionally — survived ten. That gap between the knockdown in R4 and the final bell is the thing to watch when Spence film comes out.

Sam Goodman came in having lost his WBA featherweight title shot to Nick Ball in August 2025. This was the path back. The IBF eliminator against Rodrigo Ruiz — ranked No. 10 at 122 — gave Goodman a chance to reclaim mandatory status at a different sanctioning body. He took it, though not without early trouble.

Ruiz hurt him with a left hand in the second round. The 115-113 scorecard tells you at least one judge thought it was close throughout. Goodman's footwork kept him out of sustained danger and he pulled away in the championship rounds. He is now the IBF mandatory at super bantamweight and back in a title position. The question is which belt, which opponent, and which promoter makes the call.

Spotlight Delivered — Callum Peters
Pre-Fight Spotlight · Thesis Confirmed
Callum Peters
6-0 · 6 KOs · Middleweight · Adelaide, Australia · 23 years old · 2022 Commonwealth Games Silver · 2024 Paris Olympian

The pre-fight program put Peters in the Spotlight with a specific question: what does he do when something goes wrong against a man with similar power? Delio Mouzinho came in 4-0 with four stoppages. Both men had 100% KO rates. The Blotter was watching for the first test.

Peters answered it in the second round. TKO2 at 0:57. Six fights, six stoppages, none of them past the second round. The amateur foundation is documented: 2022 Commonwealth Games silver medal at Birmingham, 2024 Paris Olympics. He is 23 years old and has never been seriously tested yet — but the pedigree is real and the results are clean.

Formal Prospect Watch entry is pending weight discipline verification. Peters goes on the active filing list. He fights in Australia on Prime Video, which means the casual global fan has not found him yet. That is the exact condition the Prospect Watch exists to document.

Prospect Watch · Formal Entry Pending
Callum Peters
6-0 · 6 KOs · Middleweight · Adelaide, Australia · Born November 25, 2002 · 23 years old

Amateur background is the most important predictor of professional ceiling under this framework. Peters' is documented and significant: 2022 Commonwealth Games silver medal at Birmingham, 2024 Paris Olympics competitor at 80kg middleweight. That is a legitimate international pedigree — not regional, not developmental. Olympic-level.

Six professional fights. Six stoppages. No fight past the second round. The opposition has been developmental — Mouzinho is the toughest test he has faced — but the manner of the wins is consistent. He does not let opportunities go. He finds the finish and takes it. That instinct, combined with the amateur foundation, is what puts him in this column.

Weight discipline check required before formal filing. Once that is confirmed clean, Peters becomes Vol. I Entry No. 6. He fights in Australia on Prime Video. The casual fan in the United States has not found him. That is the condition this column was built to catch.

Blotter Grade
B−
Vol. I · No. 6 · Tszyu-Nurja · Wollongong · April 5, 2026
Tszyu won every round and looked in control throughout. A 100-88 shutout is a dominant result on paper. But not finishing a man who had never been past six rounds professionally — after putting him on the canvas in the fourth — is the kind of gap that matters heading into a Spence fight. The co-main delivered in Goodman's eliminator win and Peters was everything the Spotlight projected. The undercard is solid. The main event told us Tszyu can win at this level. It did not tell us he can close when it counts. That answer is still outstanding.
