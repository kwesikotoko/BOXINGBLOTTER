The Read — Main Event
⚑ Blotter Flag · Narrative Spine
Steve Rolls connects both halves of this fight. Berlanga decisioned him in 2022; Butler ended him in one round in 2024. Same man, two very different nights — and tonight's winner is the one whose version of that data point holds up.
Berlanga Needs This More Than the Betting Line Admits
A two-fight losing streak, one of them a first-ever stoppage, meets a Canadian veteran who's finally stringing wins together against opposition that hasn't tested him. This is a get-right fight dressed up as a co-headline slot.

Berlanga is 23-2. Both losses came in his last two outings — a wide decision to Canelo Alvarez in September 2024, then a five-round stoppage loss to Hamzah Sheeraz in July 2025 that marked the first time he'd ever been off his feet and finished. The PFORI trend on the men he's actually beaten is mixed to negative: Rolls and Coceres both trended down afterward, while McCrory has gone 2-1 since (including a June 2026 win) — his resume reads softer in hindsight than it did in the moment, but not uniformly so. This is a man who needs a clean, visible win more than his ledger suggests.

Butler is 38-5-1, unbeaten in four straight since a June 2024 stoppage loss to Patrice Volny. But every name in that streak — Ivan Alvarez, Fernando Farias, Jonathan Gonzalez-Ortiz, Jose de Jesus Macias, Ramadan Hiseni — is opportunity-level, not resume-building. All five of his career losses have come by stoppage, so the chin question travels with him into every step-up fight, and this is a real step up.

Physically it's close: Berlanga has the two-inch height edge, but Butler actually owns more reach at 75" to 72.8". Both fight orthodox. Neither man is walking in with a clear physical advantage — this gets decided by whose recent form is more real.

Watch for whether Berlanga fights patient behind the jab or reverts to the pressure-brawling that got him stopped by Sheeraz. Watch whether Butler's chin — untested at this level since Alimkhanuly in 2023 — holds against a bigger puncher. The live scenario: Butler's power has finished sturdier operators than his recent opposition suggests, and if Berlanga is still carrying any hesitation from the Sheeraz knockdowns, Butler is live to add a signature win to a comeback résumé the Blotter otherwise wouldn't have flagged as live.

Blotter Call · Main Event · Moderate Confidence
Edgar Berlanga — Unanimous Decision
Berlanga remains the more live operator at 168 even off two losses, and Butler's recent opposition hasn't proven enough to trust the streak fully. Watch condition: if Berlanga shows any of the hesitation that let Sheeraz walk him down, this flips to a live Butler stoppage scenario given the chin questions on both sides.

The Blotter Spotlight — Co-Main
⚡ Scout's Chair — The Fighter You Need to Know
Richardson Hitchins
20-0 · 8 KOs · Brooklyn, NY (fights out of Hackensack, NJ) · Age 28 · Orthodox

Hitchins is unbeaten and a former IBF junior welterweight champion, but the number that actually matters tonight is 40 — his knockout rate, low for a fighter operating at this level. He wins on boxing skill: a 12-round unanimous decision over Jose Zepeda to claim the WBC Silver and WBO NABO belts in 2023, a split decision over Liam Paro for the IBF title in December 2024, and an eighth-round stoppage of former lightweight champion George Kambosos Jr. in June 2025.

Tonight is his welterweight debut, and it's not a soft landing on paper — he walks in already ranked WBA-5 and IBF-3 at 147. Salas is a legitimate operator, ranked WBO-11 among the sanctioning bodies, with a 75% knockout rate that dwarfs Hitchins' own. If Hitchins is only a boxer and not a puncher, moving up in weight against a live puncher is exactly the test that exposes it.

But the matchmaking behind it is deliberate. Hitchins vacated the IBF 140lb title in April rather than keep boiling down for a division he'd outgrown, signed with the newly formed Zuffa Boxing, and Salas is the calculated soft landing before what his camp is calling a title shot within 12 months. Gervonta Davis publicly needled him over the opponent level; Hitchins fired back, noting the seven-figure purse attached to the fight. It's a fair counter-point for the larger question hanging over this generation: is this really the best available fight, or is it the safest one a new promotion could build around its hometown headliner?

The ceiling question: Hitchins has never lost, but he's also never had to prove he can hurt anyone bigger than himself. Salas is the first man who can punish him for finding out the hard way.

Full Record Obligation in its purest form: Derevyanchenko's 16-6 mark undersells him completely — his chin has never been the question, his level of opposition has been elite.
