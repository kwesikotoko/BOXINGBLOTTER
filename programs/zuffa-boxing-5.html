The Read — Main Event
⚑ Blotter Flag Cortes is undefeated, trained with Mayweather, and fights in his hometown — and the betting markets still favor Garcia. That tension is the story.
The Man Who Earned the Underdog Tag
Eridson Garcia fought four times in 2025. He went somewhere new each time and won each time. That resume built a legitimate case. Now he's asking for the undefeated man's record in the undefeated man's city.

Andres Cortes has never lost a professional fight. He has won 24 times. He grew up in Las Vegas and trained in Floyd Mayweather's gym. Tonight he headlines his own card in his own town on a national platform — and the oddsmakers say he is the underdog. That is not an insult. That is earned.

Eridson Garcia earned that favorite tag fight by fight. Four wins in 2025 — including an upset of Taiga Imananga in Riyadh in December, on the biggest stage Garcia had ever touched. His only loss came in a split decision to Mukhammad Shekhov in July of that year — a fight where both fighters hit the canvas. He has been tested and he has been uncomfortable and he has come through both. That is the profile of a man who knows what hard nights feel like and is still standing at the end of them.

Cortes is fighting at 135 pounds for the first time tonight. His last two fights were at 132 catchweight. Three pounds in boxing can be marginal or significant depending on the fighter's frame. It is worth watching how Cortes moves in the first two rounds — whether he looks natural or adjusted.

The home crowd is real. The Meta Apex is not the MGM Grand, but the fighters Cortes knows, the fans who have watched him come up — they will be there. That atmosphere matters in a fight where the margin is thin. Garcia has won on the road before. But the road in Las Vegas on Easter Sunday with an undefeated local headlining is a specific kind of pressure test. We will see who Garcia is when the city is against him.

Blotter Call · Main Event

Activity edge, road warrior profile, and recent high-level competition all point to Garcia. Cortes's undefeated record and home floor are real factors. This is a split-decision fight waiting to happen — watch rounds 7-10 for who takes the championship stretch.

The Blotter Spotlight — Co-Main
⚡ Scout's Chair — The Fighter You Need to Know
Feargal "Fearless" McCrory
17-1 · 9 KOs · Coalisland, Ireland · Fighting out of Brooklyn, New York

The name on the marquee is Mark Magsayo. The name you need to remember is Feargal McCrory.

Here is what McCrory has done in professional boxing: He went to Washington D.C. for a world title shot against Lamont Roach — Roach being the man who would go on to fight Gervonta Davis and earn a controversial draw — and he got knocked down three times. He kept getting up. He lost in round eight and he left the ring with his head up. Nine months later he walked into Madison Square Garden against Keenan Carbajal and got knocked down hard in the fourth round. Most fighters go home on the canvas at MSG after a world title loss. McCrory shuffled up, checked his legs, and finished Carbajal in round eight. That is who this man is.

He signed a three-year deal with Zuffa Boxing. He flew to Las Vegas. He accepted a fight against a fighter who is a -1400 betting favorite. When asked about it, his response was: "He doesn't go back and I don't go back — it's going to be chaos in the middle of the ring." McCrory is not here to survive. He is here to fight.

The Blotter names McCrory the fighter to watch on this card. Not because the Blotter thinks he beats Magsayo — the power differential makes that genuinely difficult. Because the kind of fighter who takes the hardest available fight and calls it a blessing is the reason this seat exists.

Co-Main · Lightweight · 10 Rounds
Magsayo vs. McCrory
Former WBC Champion · Division Debut
Mark Magsayo
28-2 · 18 KOs · Philippines
Former WBC featherweight champion · Moving up from 126 to 135 · Orthodox · 30 yrs
vs
Feargal McCrory
17-1 · 9 KOs · Ireland/Brooklyn
Southpaw · Natural 135 pounder · Got up from the canvas at MSG and won · 33 yrs
Magsayo won the WBC featherweight title and is now jumping two full divisions from his championship weight. He has admitted that cutting to 126 sapped his strength and legs. The central question: does his power carry to 135, or does he arrive at a weight class where McCrory is the naturally bigger, stronger man? If Magsayo's right hand lands clean, this ends early. If it doesn't, McCrory has the engine and the crisis execution to make it a very long and uncomfortable night for the former champion.
Blotter Call Magsayo — TKO Rds 5-8 Power should carry. McCrory's heart guarantees it goes rounds. Watch how McCrory responds the first time he gets hurt.

De la Cerda vs. Fuller III
Undefeated vs. Undefeated
Alexis De la Cerda
8-0 · 4 KOs
100+ amateur fights · Sparred Chocolatito · 2 weeks notice · Federal Way, WA
vs
Ervin Fuller III
12-0 · 6 KOs
Fort Washington, MD · Unsigned · No documented amateur background
The professional records say Fuller is more experienced. The career records say the opposite. De la Cerda brings over 100 amateur fights into this building — including sparring sessions with Chocolatito Gonzalez that convinced him he belonged at a high level. Fuller has 12 professional wins and an amateur background with no documented depth. De la Cerda also accepted this fight on two weeks' notice, stepping in for an injured opponent and putting his undefeated record on the line for his Zuffa debut without a full camp. That is the Volunteer profile. The man with the shorter professional record is the more seasoned fighter. The Blotter corrects its initial read.
Blotter Call — Corrected De la Cerda — Decision 100+ amateur fights, Chocolatito sparring, two weeks notice. The experience edge belongs to De la Cerda. Fuller's upside is real — watch him in adversity.
Lightweight · 8 Rounds · Prelim Feature
Meriwether III vs. Hirsch
10-0 vs. 6-0-2 · Both Unbeaten
Robert Meriwether III
10-0 · 4 KOs · "King"
Las Vegas · 20 yrs old · DLX Boxing Gym · Former Mayweather Promotions protégé · No documented amateur background
vs
Tony Hirsch
6-0-2 · 4 KOs
Lightweight · Two draws — has been in adversity · No documented amateur background
Neither fighter has a documented amateur background the Blotter can verify — calling either man more experienced on pro record alone would repeat the same mistake made in the De la Cerda-Fuller analysis. What the records do tell us: Meriwether has more professional rounds but Hirsch has two draws, meaning he has been in fights close enough to go either way and kept his composure through them. That is not nothing. Most undefeated prospects have never faced a moment where the fight was genuinely in doubt. Hirsch has faced it twice. How Meriwether handles that kind of resistance — a man who doesn't wilt — is the real question this fight answers.
Blotter Call Meriwether III — Decision Volume and activity edge. But Hirsch's draw experience is the analytical wildcard. Watch how Meriwether responds when Hirsch refuses to fold.

He doesn't get stopped easily. This should be action from round one.
