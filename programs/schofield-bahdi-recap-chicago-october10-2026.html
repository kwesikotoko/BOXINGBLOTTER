Game Story
⚑ Blotter Flag · Narrative Spine
The program called Schofield by decision and asked whether either man could show he is built for the top of the division. Schofield won the decision. Neither man answered the question.
He Got the Belt. We’re Still Asking Questions.
Floyd Schofield III, 24, is the WBA lightweight champion after beating Lucas Bahdi, 32, in a fight the WBA ordered once Gervonta Davis went into Champion in Recess status. Two unbeaten contenders, one belt, and a combined 28 months of ring rust.

Fifteen months away, and Floyd Schofield looked like he never left. He came out of the corner bouncing and threw 41 punches in round one, more than he would throw in any round the rest of the night, while Lucas Bahdi spent three minutes trying to find him.

Round three is when it got loud. Bahdi, the heavy-handed one, ran into a check left hook and hit the canvas. That was Schofield at his best: quick feet, quick hands, and a punch that arrived before Bahdi knew it was coming. For a few rounds it looked like a coronation.

Then Bahdi started getting his licks in, and that is the part we can’t shake. He found Schofield clean in the sixth and again in the ninth, when a knee touched the canvas and nothing was scored. The jab that carried Schofield early went missing late, and by the eleventh he was throwing it and landing none of eight. Every time Schofield got loose, he handed Bahdi a way in.

The eleventh brought the strangest moment of the night. Bahdi landed while stepping on Schofield’s foot, Schofield went down, and it was scored a knockdown. Ringside saw tangled feet. It didn’t change the outcome: Schofield out-landed Bahdi by 43 punches and the judges were with him, Dave DeJonge 116-110, Alfredo Gallardo 119-108 and Steve Weisfeld 115-111, with Ben Rodriguez refereeing. We had it 115-112. He is the WBA champion. Whether he looked like one is a different conversation.

The Moment
Round 6 · Bahdi lands on Schofield against the ropes · Wintrust Arena
Round 6
Schofield’s back is to the ropes and Bahdi lands clean, in the round his numbers peaked: 10 of 30 overall and 10 of 23 power. It was also Schofield’s most accurate jab round, 5 of 9. The fight in one round: what worked for him and what kept getting through to him, in the same three minutes.
Player Verdicts
Floyd Schofield IIIDelivered
The program leaned Schofield by decision and it came in: unanimous, with a knockdown and 43 more landed punches. The three things it asked him to show split. He started fast after the layoff. He did not hold the jab through the late rounds, and Bahdi hit him cleanly too often for a fighter whose edge is speed and movement. Crisis Execution stays UNVERIFIED.
Lucas BahdiDelivered
The program said his only route was one shot after losing rounds, and he was dangerous on those terms: a scored knockdown in round eleven, his best rounds in the sixth and ninth, and 74 landed against a faster man. He was also out-landed in eight rounds and won only the ninth. He showed the counter, not a way to win rounds without it.
Sergio MendozaExceeded
The program leaned Sandoval by decision and kept Mendoza’s power as the live risk. Mendoza took the fight to a stoppage in round five, in his U.S. debut, on a card he joined as the replacement. He stole the show.
Ricardo SandovalMissed
The program leaned on his bigger wins and 167 pro rounds to 115. Neither mattered against a taller, longer southpaw who put him down in round five and ended it at 1:46.

Schofield · Bahdi
BOXING BLOTTER’s VERDICT: NOT READY

Neither fighter looked elite. The belt was a WBA title, but neither Schofield nor Bahdi showed he belongs on the level of the division’s other champions: Zepeda, Stevenson and Mason. The Blotter leaves this fight with more questions than answers.

Schofield is extremely athletic. His speed, movement and reflexes are good, and he is always bouncing. But Bahdi is a straightforward fighter with average speed, and he hit Schofield far too often and too cleanly. Schofield also has a bad habit of throwing punches while he is in the air, mid-bounce, and he drops his hands too much. Against elite opposition like Zepeda or Shakur Stevenson, that is a real problem.

Bahdi earned his relevance by beating Sylve as a big underdog and by winning another fight that could have gone either way. What we saw in the ring was a contender. The Blotter sees two contenders here, not crossover material, and both men’s personal stories are more impressive than how they looked in the ring.

One fair caveat: both fighters had been out of the ring for more than a year, and Schofield for 15 months. That context matters, but it adds to the questions instead of answering them. We will revisit both fighters’ Grind and Climb placements in the next volume.

Mendoza · Oscar Collazo
Collazo Pulled Out. Mendoza Stepped In and Stole the Show.

Oscar Collazo withdrew from the Sandoval fight on September 18, about three weeks out, and said afterward that he still wanted to fight Sandoval later. Mendoza, the replacement, has now stopped Sandoval. The Blotter doubts Collazo wants any part of Mendoza, who is very tall and long for 112 pounds.

Here is the Blotter’s condition. If Collazo’s next opponent is anyone other than Taduran, Mendoza or Sandoval, we will call it a MAJOR DUCK!, remove him from the P4P Radar, and side-eye any publication that still lists him.

Flores · Rivera · Harvey
Three Prospects, Three Wins

Grant Flores’s round-two stoppage meets the program’s own line for a step toward a ranked opponent. Rivera won 99-89 on all three cards with a knockdown, and Harvey stopped an inactive man in three. The open question for both is who they face when the opposition stops being a stepping stone.
