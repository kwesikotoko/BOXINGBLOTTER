The Read — Main Event
⚑ Opposition Audit — Denis Nurja
20-0 with 9 stoppages. Never past 6 rounds professionally. Most wins recorded in Albania, Italy, and neighboring countries. Highest-level opponent: Charles Shinima (previously unbeaten). One fight was stopped when fans entered the ring and attacked his opponent — that result stands in the record. WBA ranked #12 at 154lb. The Blotter notes this not as a dismissal but as an honest framing: tonight is a genuine step up, and we will learn something real about Nurja that his record cannot tell us.
Tszyu's Resurrection. Nurja's Ceiling. Wollongong Decides Both.
The crowd knows Tszyu. The crowd has always known Tszyu. Tonight they meet a man who has been winning quietly in corners of the world where nobody was watching — and tonight, everyone is watching.

Tim Tszyu's last five fights tell a complicated story. He lost his WBC super welterweight title to Sebastian Fundora in a split decision — a fight the Blotter has documented as legitimate. He was then stopped by Bakhram Murtazaliev. He lost to Fundora again in a rematch stopped in round seven. In between those losses he won twice against developmental opposition. 2-3 in five fights for a former world champion is the record of a man in crisis — or a man in process. Tonight is supposed to tell us which.

The new training camp with Pedro Diaz in Miami is the story within the story. Diaz has rebuilt fighters before. The question is not whether Tszyu's skills are still there — they are — but whether his chin and his confidence have survived the back-to-back losses to Fundora. A fighter who has been stopped twice in two years carries that into the ring whether he acknowledges it or not. Nurja, who has never been hurt by a quality fighter, will not know what Tszyu knows. That experience differential cuts both ways.

Wollongong is fighting territory. This is where Alex Volkanovski built his base. The crowd that fills the WIN Entertainment Centre tonight is not coming to watch a tune-up. They are coming to see a former champion prove himself again in a city that believed in him before the losses. That atmosphere is a factor. For a fighter rebuilding his confidence, a hostile-crowd performance is worth less than a performance in a room that loves you. Tszyu has that tonight.

The Blotter's call: Tszyu by TKO in rounds 5-9. Nurja's opposition record does not prepare him for Tszyu's power and volume at this level. What the Blotter watches for: how Tszyu responds if Nurja catches him clean. The style change under Diaz will be visible in the first three rounds. If Tszyu looks sharper and more composed, the path to Spence becomes real. If he looks anxious, the losses have left a mark that camp didn't erase.

The Blotter Spotlight — The Discovery
⚡ Scout's Chair — Find Him Now
Callum Peters
5-0 · 5 KOs · 2024 Australian Olympian · Pro debut: December 2024

Callum Peters has been a professional boxer for sixteen months. He turned pro in December 2024 with a first-round stoppage. He now has five fights and five stoppages and is fighting on Prime Video in his home country in front of the biggest crowd of his career so far.

He competed at the 2024 Paris Olympics for Australia. He is 25 years old. He has a 100% stoppage rate across sixteen professional months. The Blotter is at this seat precisely for this fighter in this moment — before the promotional machinery arrives, before the hype cycle builds, when it's still just a young man with a dream and a record and a question mark.

Peters fights Delio Mouzinho of Timor-Leste tonight — also 4-0 with four stoppages. Two fighters with 100% KO rates who have never been past the second round professionally, matched against each other on a Prime Video card. The Blotter is not here to hype Peters. It is here to watch him. Can he handle pressure from a man with similar power? What does he do when something goes wrong? The answers to those questions are worth more than the record.

If Peters continues developing at his current trajectory, this card is one of his first footprints. Remember where you saw it.

Wollongong is explicitly called Sam Goodman's territory in the promotional materials — alongside Alex Volkanovski. He fights here as the local hero with an IBF mandatory title shot as the prize. Ruiz is the fighter the Blotter wants to watch. 23-1 with 17 stoppages — he doesn't leave outcomes to judges willingly. His only loss came in a split decision to Shekhov in a fight where both men were knocked down. He has traveled from Argentina and won before. He is not here to survive. The 74% KO rate against 23 opponents means he has a finishing instinct that goes beyond just being powerful. This fight has a stoppage on both sides of the ledger.
Blotter Call Goodman — Decision Home territory and natural weight. Ruiz's KO rate makes every round dangerous. Watch for the third act — Ruiz finishes fights late.

Do not look past Mouzinho. He has stopped two previously unbeaten fighters in 2025 — one in Canada, one in Australia. He traveled to Australia and stopped a man on his home soil. That alone separates him from most 4-0 fighters. Combined this matchup has 9 stoppages in 9 professional fights. Neither man has been past the second round. Neither man has been tested by a fighter with similar power. Six rounds of answers coming on Easter Sunday on Prime Video.
Blotter Call Peters — TKO Rds 3-5 Home crowd, Olympic pedigree, slight experience edge. Mouzinho's road warrior record makes him dangerous. Blotter's key question: how does Peters handle the first time he gets hit by someone with real power?
Heavyweight · Crossover Watch
Asofa-Solomona vs. Wallace
Second Pro Fight · NRL Crossover
Nelson Asofa-Solomona is 6'7" and 1-0 with one stoppage as a professional. He is a crossover attraction from NRL rugby league and will be one of the most-watched men on the card despite being the least experienced. The Blotter notes this honestly: the Blotter is not equipped to grade a second professional fight. What the Blotter can say is that the crowd is here for him and the pressure of performance on a Prime Video card in front of your own fans in your second pro fight is something most fighters never have to handle. Whether he is ready for that is the real question.
