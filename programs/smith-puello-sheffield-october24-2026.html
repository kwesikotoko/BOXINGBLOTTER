Title Status (Rule 62)
WBC super lightweight title · mandatory defense Smith won the belt on January 10, 2026, stopping Matias in round five at the Barclays Center in New York. This is his first defense, and it is an obligation: Puello is the WBC's ordered challenger after the council turned down his appeal for an immediate Matias rematch. The date was originally June 6; Smith withdrew with an elbow injury on April 27.
The Read — Main Event
⚑ Blotter Flag · Narrative Spine
Puello and Smith share a common opponent in Subriel Matias. Puello lost a majority decision to him, with two judges at 115-113 and one at 114-114. Smith stopped him in five. The fight is whether Smith's answer to a man who comes at you carries over to a man who won't be standing where you're swinging.
Matias Walked Forward. Puello Won't.
One champion has a belt, a home crowd and a long layoff. The other has been in with the best of the division, has never been stopped, and has had three straight twelve-round title fights decided by the judges.

Smith is the Sheffield kid who did it the long way. British title, Commonwealth title, a WBC Silver belt won by knocking out José Zepeda in five in this same arena, then a twelve-round decision over Mathieu Germain before the Matias fight made him a world champion in New York. He came out of the amateurs with an 88-10 record, a 2014 European Youth silver, a 2015 Commonwealth Youth Games bronze and the 2018 ABA light welterweight title. His father, Grant, trains him. He has not fought since January, nine and a half months of waiting after the elbow injury cost him the June date.

Puello, born in the Dominican Republic and based in Las Vegas, is the other kind of champion, the one who earned every inch of it with his feet. He won the WBA title in 2022 and the WBC belt in 2024 on a split decision over Gary Antuanne Russell, then beat Sandor Martín by split decision in a defense. Matias took the belt on July 12, 2025, and did it close: 115-113 twice, 114-114 on the third card. He stayed busy on July 18 with a fourth-round stoppage of Bolano, and he has never been stopped in his career. He works with Ismael Salas.

They are close to the same size, within an inch of each other by the numbers in circulation, and the stance does the rest. Smith is orthodox, Puello a southpaw. Smith has made a habit of stopping lefties: Perouene, O'Maison and Zepeda all went before the final bell. His own line on the subject: "It is not awkward." He also calls Puello "technical and rangey," which is the polite way to say you will not find him easily.

Here is what the Matias comparison actually says. Matias is a constant pressure fighter, and Smith's straight right hand, right uppercut and body work were built to meet a man walking at him. Puello, in his own words, is "not a fighter that stays put." Smith's keys: cut the ring off instead of chasing it, and land the right hand early in the exchange, before the pivot. Puello's keys: keep the jab and the pivot going for twelve and never stand in the pocket long enough for the right hand to find him. The upset is live. Take the first six rounds on activity and angles, and the second half is a judges' fight in front of a room that is not his.

Judge's Chair
How This Gets Scored
Decision Risk: HIGH

Judges look first for clean, effective punching, then for who is forcing the pace, then ring control, with defense last. Puello's movement makes clean punches hard to count, and Smith's heavier shots count for more when they land, so the first two criteria will pull against each other. The close rounds will sit in the middle of the fight, where Smith is cutting the ring and Puello is slipping out of it.

Lee Every referees, with Kieran McCann, Jon Llona and Ray Danseco scoring. Puello's last three twelve-rounders went to split or majority cards, which is the shape this one could take.

Score it yourself: if Smith is landing the right hand on the way out of Puello's pivot by round six, give him the late rounds in your head and expect to see the same card the judges do.
📋 Rule 63 · Disclosure
In April 2023 Puello tested positive for clomiphene. The WBA stripped him of its title and the Nevada commission suspended him for six months. He has said the drug had been prescribed after surgery and was not declared.

The Blotter Spotlight — Leo Atang
⚡ Scout's Chair — The Fighter You Need to Know
LEO ATANG
8-0 · 7 KOs · York · Age 19 · Heavyweight

Atang is 19 years old and 6'6", and he has already stopped seven of his eight opponents. The part the casual fan misses is the amateur résumé behind it: he medaled at the 2023 European Junior Championships, taking silver, and then turned pro on a path that put him on the Blotter's Approach roster as an 8-0 heavyweight after he stopped Glen Williams in two rounds on his US debut in August. Sunny Edwards co-manages him.

Tonight he fights Jonathan Exequiel Vergara (9-4) over six rounds on the Smith–Puello card. The question for a teenage heavyweight is never the knockout. It is whether he stays calm when a man who has been hit before keeps coming forward, and six rounds is a good place to find out.

Rule 46 · Fighter Environment
Environment Analysis · Utilita Arena Sheffield
Smith: This is his city. His father is in the corner, he won the WBC Silver belt in this room, and the crowd has waited since January. The same layoff that makes him rusty means nine and a half months with no distraction but this fight.

Puello: A road fight, and he knows it. He stayed busy in July against Bolano, which is the opposite of Smith's year. He does not have to win the crowd; he has to win the rounds clearly enough that the cards cannot be argued.
