Game Story
⚑ Blotter Flag · Narrative Spine
The call was Roach on recent form against tougher opposition. Zepeda's output, not Roach's résumé, decided it.
When Enough Was Enough
Zepeda took the vacant WBC lightweight title from Roach by unanimous decision, out-throwing him by roughly 300 punches over 12 rounds.

Roach banked the opening round — his best of the night by most reads. From Round 2 on, Zepeda started rolling downhill, and Roach's defense, genuinely sound all night, couldn't keep pace with the math: whatever points clean guarding earned him were being buried under Zepeda's volume.

Zepeda didn't just walk through him, either. He boxed — real footwork, real range-finding between flurries, not pure pressure. By Round 9 he'd already landed more total punches than any previous Roach opponent, with three rounds still to go.

It went the full 12. Final CompuBox: Zepeda out-landed Roach 219-153 overall, 77-29 in jabs, 142-124 in power shots, 62-29 to the body. Scorecards were competitive on paper (117-111 twice, 116-113) but the fight was not — the Blotter's own live scorecard had it the same way, 117-111, Zepeda taking 9 of the 12 rounds.

The main event is the whole story here — Roach was the headline pick and lost clearly. The pre-fight Trainer Consistency check came back a wash for both camps and didn't flag this; the real signal missed was weighting Zepeda's work-rate and output more heavily against a clean but lower-volume counter-punching style. Lesson for the file: when a proven high-volume pressure fighter meets a technically sound, lower-output boxer, treat the output gap itself as a live risk factor in the call, not just a stylistic footnote.
William Zepeda
A Real Problem at 135

First world title. His lone loss remains Shakur Stevenson (July 2025), and outside of Stevenson, the Blotter doesn't see many who beat him right now. Abdullah Mason, the WBO titleholder, is the fight to want next — and the one worth finding out about.

Lamont Roach Jr.
No More Ambiguity

Second career loss, and his first since a 2019 decision to Jamel Herring — no draw to hide behind this time after back-to-back majority draws against Gervonta Davis and Isaac Cruz. Open question now is whether he rebuilds at 135 or looks elsewhere.

The Lightweight Landscape
Two Belts Could Come Loose

Muratalla flagged after the fight that 135 pounds has become a hard make and this may have been his last fight there — a likely IBF vacancy. Add in Gervonta Davis's inactivity since the Roach draw (Roach has fought twice since; Davis hasn't fought at all) and two of the division's four belts could functionally be up for grabs soon, even as Zepeda (WBC) and Mason (WBO) give the division its two clearest names.
