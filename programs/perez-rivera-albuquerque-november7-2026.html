The Read — Main Event
⚑ Blotter Flag · Narrative Spine
Both men have already boxed Jonathan Gonzalez, and the cards read differently. Perez won a split decision with two judges at 115-113; Rivera lost all three cards, one of them by a single point. The champion fights at home with his father in the corner, and his stamina is the open question after the near-drowning that stopped his heart in July 2024.
THE GONZALEZ LINE
Common-opponent math is a hint, not a proof. Perez has the belt, the building and the better paper result; Rivera has an Olympic résumé and the stance changes to make a hometown crowd uneasy.

Abraham Perez is 15-0 with seven knockouts, 27 years old, born in El Paso and living in Albuquerque. He holds the WBA interim flyweight title after beating Jonathan Gonzalez on June 14 by split decision, 115-113 on two cards and 115-113 for Gonzalez on the third. His father, Aaron Perez, trains him and co-heads Legacy Boxing Promotions. In July 2024 a near-drowning briefly stopped Perez's heart, and the recovery cost him stamina; he is 5-0 since, including a unanimous decision over Domingo on April 4.

Yankiel Rivera Figueroa is 8-1-1 with four knockouts, 29, from Bayamón. He was Puerto Rico's only boxer at the 2020 Olympics, finishing a 133-14 amateur career with a 2019 Pan American Games bronze. As a pro he drew by majority decision with Cordova in August 2025, lost to Gonzalez by unanimous decision on January 3 (111-116, 110-117, 113-114), and stopped Francisco Vargas Martinez on September 11.

The Gonzalez fights are the only line between them. Perez's cards were closer than Rivera's, but Perez beat Gonzalez five months after Rivera lost to him, and a fighter is not the same man in June as in January. Neither man is a heavy puncher: Perez has stopped seven of fifteen, Rivera four of ten, so the likeliest ending is the scorecards.

Perez's camp expects a chess match that goes the distance, and Perez describes Rivera as a pressure fighter who also counters and feints. Rivera switches stances and boxed mostly as a southpaw against Gonzalez. The Blotter is watching two things: whether Perez's legs hold from the ninth round on, and whether Rivera's stance changes cost Perez his timing in the middle rounds.

Judge's Chair
How This Gets Scored
Decision Risk: HIGH

Rivera's fights with Cordova and Gonzalez produced a draw and a one-point card; Perez's title win produced a split. Expect close rounds decided by who lands the cleaner shot in a fifty-fifty exchange, and by who is seen as the aggressor. Pressure scores with some judges and counters score with others, and this fight offers both.

Score it yourself: if Rivera's switches leave Perez throwing second in the middle rounds, you are looking at a Rivera card no matter how Perez finishes.
📋 Amateur Thread — Two Olympic-Track Résumés
Perez won the U.S. Olympic Trials and the 2019 Golden Gloves national flyweight title, and boxed as an amateur in Bulgaria, Spain and France. Rivera reached the Tokyo Games for Puerto Rico and won a Pan American Games bronze in 2019. They come from different amateur systems, and nothing places them in the same ring before tonight.
Perez retains if
  • He controls distance in rounds one through four and banks rounds before the stance changes start.
  • His legs and breathing are still sharp in rounds nine through twelve.
  • He lands first when Rivera steps in from the switch, rather than answering after.
Rivera wins if
  • His pressure turns into volume over the final third.
  • The stance changes break Perez's timing and leave him a half-beat late.
  • He takes the close rounds on activity; one card had him one point from Gonzalez.

How to Read the Blotter Mark

Each Call sits on a nine-slot line. Our pick is always on the left; his opponent is on the right; the middle slot is a draw. The shaded slots are our mark, where we think the fight lands. After the fight, the recap puts a second marker on the line where the ring actually landed, and counts the steps between the two. The closer the marks, the better the Call.

⚑ Blotter Flag · A World Title Fight on the Prelims
Pedro Taduran (20-4-1, 14 KOs) defends the IBF minimumweight title against Brandon Moreno (16-0, 5 KOs) on the Wynn Records Network prelims, hours before the DAZN main card goes live at 8 PM ET. The Blotter thinks that is the wrong slot for a reigning world champion. It is also the fight of the man we largely suspect Oscar Collazo (15-0, 12 KOs) has been steering around. The full case follows.
The World Title Fight on the Prelims
Pedro Taduran
⚡ Scout's Chair — The Champion on the Prelims
PEDRO TADURAN
20-4-1 · 14 KOs · Libon, Albay, Philippines · Age 30 · Southpaw · Trainer Joven Jimenez

Taduran is in his second reign as IBF minimumweight champion and fighting his fourth defense. It is a mandatory defense because the top-rated Zhu Dianxing was unavailable, and Moreno is rated fourth. His regular trainer, Carl Peñalosa, was denied a U.S. visa, so Jimenez leads the corner with Jerwin Ancajas assisting.

The Blotter's position: Taduran is the fighter we largely suspect Oscar Collazo is avoiding. Collazo is 29, 15-0 with twelve knockouts, and a pound-for-pound candidate on many analysts' lists. The record is public. Taduran's camp has pursued a unification since the fall of 2025. Collazo's last two fights were a sixth-round stoppage of Jesus Haro in March and a second-round stoppage of Neider Valdez in June, a late-replacement bout at flyweight; he has since called for Ricardo Sandoval at 112. Collazo says he wants the biggest fights and counts Taduran among the best. We read the pattern, not the words. It is our opinion and we are labeling it as one.

Taduran needs more shine. A defense on the prelims is not the platform a fight with a pound-for-pound candidate needs, and this one is a world title fight that could have headlined a card.

PRESSURE VERSUS DISTANCE
Nobody has stopped Taduran in 25 fights, and Moreno has never been outside Spain. The fight is whether a champion who finishes can catch a challenger who goes the distance.

Taduran is the harder and better-tested fighter, and the market says so. He won the title back by stopping Ginjiro Shigeoka in the ninth round in Japan, then beat him again in the rematch by split decision, with one judge for Shigeoka after a late rally. He outpointed Christian Balunan in October and stopped Alvarez in the seventh round. Fourteen of his twenty wins came inside the distance, and none of his four losses was a stoppage.

Moreno is 16-0 with five knockouts, and nine of his last ten went the distance. His camp talks about movement over power. He survived a knockdown to win the Spanish title against Raul Zabala, which is the best evidence he can take a punch. This is his first fight outside Spain and his first against a champion with three world-level defenses.

The wrinkle is Taduran's corner. Peñalosa has been with him since 2024, and Jimenez's methods are new; Taduran has said blending them is a work in progress. The Shigeoka rematch shows how a fighter beats the champion's pressure: stay out of range early and rally late.

Taduran retains if
  • He cuts the ring early, so Moreno never settles into a rhythm.
  • He goes to the body and takes Moreno's legs by the middle rounds.
  • He keeps pressing in rounds ten through twelve rather than coasting on the cards.
Moreno wins if
  • He makes Taduran miss and counters out of the exchanges.
  • He survives the first four rounds without being backed to the ropes.
  • He wins the late rounds the way Shigeoka nearly did.
Blotter Call · Prelim World Title Fight · Lean
PEDRO TADURAN — TKO
Taduran has fourteen stoppages and has never been stopped, and the market has him near 11 to 1. Moreno's nine-in-ten distance record and the knockdown he survived hold the Call to Lean under Rule 29. Look for rounds eight to eleven. If Moreno is still moving freely after the sixth, this becomes a long night.

The Blotter Spotlight — Co-Main
⚡ Scout's Chair — The Fighter You Need to Know
ATIF OBERLTON
17-0 · 14 KOs · Philadelphia · Age 28 · Southpaw

Oberlton is on the Blotter's Grind and Climb list, in The Approach section, which tracks fighters on their own merit before outside recognition arrives. He is a 6'3" southpaw light heavyweight with a 73-inch reach and fourteen knockouts in seventeen fights, and he has stopped his recent opposition.

Tonight is another step up against a heavy puncher. Oberlton has said that going ten rounds for the first time last time out gave him more confidence, and he is on the DAZN card as the co-feature. The question is whether his height and stance can handle a man who hits as hard as Deon Nicholson.
