Game Story
⚑ Blotter Flag · Narrative Spine
The program leaned Foster by decision and said the real risk was the judges. The risk never showed up: two cards read 118-108, which is where the Blotter had it, and Foster won this one in the ring, round after round.
Foster Wins the Night and the Division
O’Shaquie Foster, 33, took the WBO and IBF belts from Emanuel Navarrete, 31, and added them to his WBC title for a three-belt unification at 130 pounds.

Navarrete walked into San Antonio to do what he always does: come forward, swarm, make the other man pay for every inch. For the first round he got nothing. Zero landed on twenty thrown, Foster’s jab already in his face, and a building that had come to see the Mexican champion work got a quiet start instead.

It stayed that way. Foster boxed from the outside, picked his spots and stepped away, and Navarrete kept coming and kept missing. He landed 19 percent of what he threw, after landing 27 percent across his last eleven fights. The jab the program told readers to watch was the story of the night: Foster’s landed at nearly twice Navarrete’s rate.

The tenth was Navarrete’s best round, and the only one where he matched Foster’s landed total, 23 apiece. Foster answered it. With the fight in hand he went for the finish in the 12th and dropped Navarrete twice with right hands. Navarrete got up both times and reached the final bell, and the scores were 118-108, 118-108 and 115-111.

The Blotter had it 118-108, the same as two of the three judges. The 115-111 card is the outlier: Foster out-landed Navarrete in eleven of twelve rounds and tied the other, and that judge still kept it within four points.

Every winner was right and three of four methods were wrong. Two of those misses came from the same habit: durability history pulled the stoppage call down a tier on Vazquez and Zaina, and a body-shot finisher and a late-round flurry got through both. The Davis call went the other way, a stoppage call for a ten-knockout fighter who won every round and never closed it. The lesson for the permanent file: a chin record lowers the odds of a stoppage, it does not rule one out against a finisher on form.
O’Shaquie Foster
Three belts, and a place in the Blotter’s top 10 pound-for-pound

Foster said afterward, “I want to become undisputed. That’s my dream.” The only 130-pound belt he does not hold is the WBA, which Anthony Cacace defends against Nick Ball on December 19. Charly Suarez is the WBO’s mandatory challenger, and Foster is expected to face him.

Albert “Chop Chop” Gonzalez
Star confirmed, and the next step is a title shot

At 19-0 with 12 KOs, Gonzalez stopped a two-time world-title challenger in two rounds. By the program’s own scale that makes him a star. Afterward he said he would take the Espinoza fight on October 31. A world-title shot at 126 is where this goes, and the Blotter’s read is that he has earned one.

Frank Martin
A debut win that left the question open

Martin is 20-1-1 with 14 KOs and holds the NABO title, but until the finish he looked ordinary against Zaina, and the win says little about where he stands at 140. His next opponent has to say more than this one did.
