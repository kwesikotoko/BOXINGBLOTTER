Game Story
The Rust Was Real. So Was the Control.
Fury outboxed Makhmudov from round three through twelve. He also never turned it up when the opportunity to finish was there. Both things happened. Both things matter.

The opening round belonged to Makhmudov. He came in fast, landed an overhand right before Fury had found his range, and pushed the former champion onto the ropes. The early round danger flag the Blotter raised in the pre-fight program was real — Makhmudov's aggression in the first three minutes was the most dangerous he would be all night. Fury looked uncomfortable. The crowd at Tottenham was nervous.

Then Fury remembered who he was. From round three onward he controlled the fight with his jab and right uppercut, walking Makhmudov onto shots at close range, landing consistently to the body, and building a lead that was never seriously in question. By round nine, CompuBox tracked Fury outlanding Makhmudov 106-44. That is not a close fight. That is a dominant performance on the punch statistics.

The problem — and the reason the Tottenham crowd went quiet — was what Fury didn't do with the lead. In the second half of the contest, on multiple occasions, Fury had Makhmudov hurt and chose not to escalate. His corner told him to go for the knockout entering the championship rounds. He worked the body and the jab instead. Makhmudov's tactic of clinching at every opportunity aided his survival, but survival was possible partly because Fury allowed it.

The Blotter called Fury TKO rounds 5-8. The winner was correct. The method was wrong. The performance was better than a near-shutout scorecard suggests and worse than the crowd expected. The honest analytical reading: Fury at 37, returning after 16 months, with two Usyk losses behind him, outboxed a man who had been stopped twice by quality heavyweights and never really looked like he belonged at this level. That is a win. What it is not is the statement performance that makes Anthony Joshua look at the Joshua fight as a risk he can afford to take.

The Moment
Round 10 — Vintage. Brief. Not Enough.
Fury spun beautifully off the ropes and unloaded a combination that landed clean — head movement, pivot, volume, timing all present simultaneously. The crowd rose. That was the fighter they came to see. It lasted one sequence. Makhmudov held and it ended. The moment was real. It was also the exception, not the rule, and the crowd knew the difference.
— The fight in one passage. The best Fury was there. It was not the whole night.

A 106-44 punch differential through nine rounds is not a close fight. Fury controlled the distance, won rounds cleanly, and outlanded his opponent by more than 2:1. The issue was never the scorecards. It was what those numbers felt like from the crowd's perspective — technically sound, physically dominant, and curiously reluctant to go for the finish.
Player Verdicts
Delivered
Tyson Fury
Won cleanly. Controlled every round after the first. Showed enough jab, enough movement, and enough ring intelligence to confirm that the core skills are still there. Did not show the finishing instinct or the sustained power that made him the best heavyweight in the world. Whether that is the 16-month layoff or something more permanent is the question that remains open. Joshua fight will be the answer.
Below Expectations
Arslanbek Makhmudov
The early danger was real. After round two, nothing. He held at every clinch, never found a clean sequence, and was largely an unwilling participant in his own performance — using survival tactics to protect his record rather than any aggressive game plan. He was stopped twice before this fight. He was not stopped tonight because Fury chose not to finish him. That distinction belongs in the analytical file.
Co-Main Result — A Volunteer's Final Night
150lb Catchweight · 10 Rounds
Conor Benn def. Regis Prograis — Unanimous Decision
Partial Hit
98-92 · 98-92 · 98-92 · Benn now 25-1 · Prograis retires 30-4
The Blotter called Benn TKO rounds 4-8. Benn won every round on every card but never pulled the trigger. The winner was correct. The method was wrong. That said, the analytical framing holds up on review: Benn's body work and jab controlled the fight. Prograis, fighting with a leg injury that was visible from the opening bell — he wobbled in round one from a right hand and never looked balanced throughout — gave a game account of himself in the middle rounds but faded as the size differential wore him down. Benn cuts over both eyes from head clashes didn't change the outcome. The 98-92 scorecards were correct. The fight was lethargic. The $15 million debut did not deliver the performance the billing demanded. The story that matters more than the scorecard: Regis Prograis announced his retirement after the fight. He applauded the decision, stepped to the microphone, and said this was his last night. The Volunteer's final act was to accept a fight against a bigger, younger, fresher opponent at a weight above his natural class, in the opponent's home country, and compete with dignity for ten rounds. He was never going to win. He went anyway. The Blueprint notes that without equivocation. That is what the Volunteer profile looks like at the end.
British Heavyweight Title — The David Haye Path Opens
British Heavyweight Title · 12 Rounds
Richard Riakporhe def. Jeamie TKV Tshikeva — TKO Round 5
Hit
Riakporhe now 20-1, 16 KOs · New British Heavyweight Champion · Stoppage at 2:12 R5 · TKV now 9-3
Called Riakporhe TKO rounds 5-9. It happened in round 5. Clean hit. The execution was exactly as the Blueprint projected — Riakporhe applied steady pressure, bloodied Tshikeva's nose by round two, received a point deduction against TKV for headbutting in round four, and finished the job in round five with a right hand that dropped the champion. Tshikeva beat the count but couldn't defend and the referee stepped in at 2:12. The David Haye comparison Riakporhe made himself — British cruiserweight champion to British heavyweight champion to WBA heavyweight world title — is now one step completed. He is a two-time British champion in two different divisions. He spoke after the fight exactly as someone who knows where the path leads: "I don't like watching long fights. I'm going to keep on training to knock people out." The Blotter watches Riakporhe's next fight with genuine analytical interest. Note on TKV: He defended his title fighting in the stadium two blocks from where he grew up. The home postcode crowd was real. Riakporhe was better. TKV's grit — the quality that beat Clarke — was present but insufficient against a man with Riakporhe's power and experience. That is not a failure of character. It is an honest result.

Lesson File — New Entries
Permanent Lesson File · April 11, 2026
A fighter returning after 16 months with two documented losses to the pound-for-pound number one, against an opponent who was stopped by two quality fighters and never went past early rounds, may win dominantly on the scorecards without showing finishing instinct. Form decline + rust + safe opponent is not the same combination as form decline + rust + dangerous opponent. The Fury call should have accounted for the type of win available — near-shutout decision rather than stopping — given Makhmudov's survival instinct and holding tactics.
A fight with a pre-confirmed leg injury on one side (Prograis, rumored and then verified by his movement throughout) changes the method projection significantly. If Benn knew he was fighting an injured Prograis, the finishing instinct to go through the gears was reduced because the submission risk was also reduced. Injury intelligence — when credibly sourced — must factor into method projections, not just winner projections.
The Volunteer profile applied to Prograis was the correct framing and was confirmed by his retirement announcement. When the analytical frame is right before the fight and confirmed by events after it, that is the Blotter doing its job. The scorecard miss doesn't erase the framing correctness.
Legacy
What Saturday Night Left Behind

Fury called out Anthony Joshua. Joshua was sitting ringside. Joshua gave the cameras a response that suggested he wasn't ready to commit. Netflix confirmed they are working on the fight. The Blueprint will have a program when it is official. The Gypsy King versus AJ — the fight British boxing has been waiting for, finally without world titles involved, both men past their peak and both with something to prove. If it happens, 62,850 at Tottenham will feel like a rehearsal.

Regis Prograis retired. He stood at the microphone at 37 years old, in a foreign country, in a fight he was never going to win, and said this was his last night. He fought Josh Taylor, Devin Haney, Jack Catterall, and Conor Benn in his final chapter. He took every hard fight available. The Volunteer profile is confirmed across a career and a retirement speech. The Boxing Blotter notes it with the respect it deserves.

Richard Riakporhe is the British heavyweight champion. He has now won British titles at cruiserweight and heavyweight. The David Haye path is real. Nobody else in the division is walking it faster.
